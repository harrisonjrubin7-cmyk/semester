# Semester: Complete Product Vision and Requirements

## Core vision

**Semester should be a complete university operating system that replaces and centralizes the functions of Canvas, Brightspace, Blackboard, Moodle, university email, calendars, registration portals, advising systems, financial accounts, and campus-service platforms.**

It should give students one secure application for managing their entire academic and campus experience—without game mechanics or unnecessary distractions.

Semester should answer three questions immediately:

1. What do I need to do?
2. What do I need to know?
3. What is happening next?

## Platforms and systems Semester should replace

| Existing platform | Functions Semester must provide |
|---|---|
| Canvas | Courses, assignments, submissions, grades, announcements, discussions, files, quizzes, and feedback |
| Brightspace | Course content, assessments, gradebooks, progress tracking, class communication, and learning analytics |
| Blackboard | Learning modules, assignments, tests, discussions, messages, grades, and course resources |
| Moodle | Courses, lessons, quizzes, forums, files, progress tracking, and instructor tools |
| University email | Inbox, sending, replies, attachments, filters, search, contact directory, and urgent-message detection |
| Calendar applications | Classes, deadlines, meetings, exams, events, reminders, scheduling, and calendar synchronization |
| Registration portals | Course search, schedule building, enrollment, waitlists, prerequisites, holds, and registration management |
| Advising systems | Advisor appointments, degree planning, academic progress, notes, forms, and major-declaration workflows |
| Financial accounts | Tuition bills, payments, financial aid, scholarships, refunds, balances, and transaction history |
| Campus-service platforms | Dining, housing, mail, transportation, libraries, recreation, health, safety, career services, and student ID |
| Separate study applications | Syllabus processing, study guides, flashcards, quizzes, notes, summaries, study plans, and exam preparation |

Semester should not merely become a collection of links. Important actions should happen directly inside Semester whenever institutional authorization permits.

# 4. Study-guide generator with 11 formats

Semester should generate study materials from syllabi, uploaded files, lecture slides, readings, notes, recordings, and course modules.

The user should be able to choose one or combine multiple formats.

## The 11 study-guide formats

1. **Comprehensive Study Guide**  
   Detailed explanations of all relevant concepts, definitions, theories, examples, formulas, and relationships.

2. **Simple Summary**  
   Short, plain-language explanations of only the most important material.

3. **Outline Format**  
   Hierarchical topics, subtopics, definitions, evidence, and examples.

4. **Bullet-Point Notes**  
   Organized, scannable notes designed for quick review.

5. **Flashcards**  
   Question-and-answer cards with optional spaced-repetition scheduling.

6. **Practice Quiz**  
   Multiple-choice, true-or-false, matching, and short-answer questions with explanations.

7. **Practice Exam**  
   A realistic timed examination based on the course materials, difficulty, and professor’s stated format.

8. **Key Terms and Definitions**  
   A glossary containing essential vocabulary, people, theories, formulas, dates, and concepts.

9. **Concept Map**  
   A visual representation showing relationships among topics and ideas.

10. **Formula and Problem-Solving Guide**  
   Equations, variables, worked examples, problem-solving steps, and common mistakes.

11. **Audio or Read-Aloud Study Guide**  
   A spoken review that students can listen to while walking, exercising, or commuting.

## Study-guide controls

Students should be able to select:

- Course
- Materials to include
- Guide format
- Length
- Difficulty
- Exam date
- Topics
- Reading level
- Number of questions
- Question types
- Time available to study
- Areas of weakness
- Whether answers appear immediately or at the end

Every generated study guide should:

- Cite its source material
- Link back to the relevant page or slide
- Avoid inventing information
- Clearly label AI-generated explanations
- Allow manual editing
- Be saved inside the course
- Export to PDF or Word
- Be printable
- Be shareable when permitted
- Regenerate selected sections without replacing the entire guide

# 14. Semester AI assistant

The AI assistant should function as an organizer, tutor, search system, and planning assistant.

It should be able to:

- Answer questions using course materials
- Explain difficult concepts
- Summarize lectures and readings
- Process uploaded syllabi
- Generate all 11 study-guide formats
- Create flashcards and practice tests
- Build study schedules
- Find deadlines
- Prioritize assignments
- Summarize announcements and emails
- Draft messages
- Identify conflicting information
- Explain degree requirements
- Guide students to campus resources

## AI safeguards

The assistant must:

- Cite the source of every factual answer
- Link to the original document
- Distinguish official information from AI suggestions
- Never invent deadlines, grades, policies, or financial information
- Respect instructor academic-integrity settings
- Protect private information
- Ask for confirmation before changing or submitting important information
- Allow institutions to configure permitted AI functions

# 17. User roles

Semester requires different interfaces and permissions for:

## Students

Courses, assignments, schedules, messages, grades, studying, finances, advising, and campus services.

## Faculty

Course creation, content publishing, assignments, grading, attendance, announcements, communication, analytics, and AI controls.

## Advisors

Academic plans, appointments, notes, degree progress, alerts, and approvals.

## Administrators

Enrollment, policies, course catalogs, billing, financial aid, reporting, integrations, security, and system configuration.

## Parents or authorized payers

Only explicitly permitted financial information and payment functions.

## Campus-service staff

Tools relevant to dining, housing, transportation, libraries, health, recreation, and other assigned services.

Access must be role-based and limited to necessary information.

# 19. Security and privacy

Because Semester would process educational, financial, identity, and potentially health-related information, it needs:

- University single sign-on
- Multi-factor authentication
- Role-based permissions
- Encryption in transit and at rest
- Secure API access
- Short-lived authentication tokens
- Detailed audit logs
- Data minimization
- Permission-management screens
- Ability to disconnect an account
- Data export and deletion tools
- Regular penetration testing
- Incident-response procedures
- Secure backups
- FERPA-conscious data handling
- Appropriate financial and health-data protections
- Transparent AI data policies

Semester should never sell student data or use private course materials to train models without explicit authorization.

# 23. What Semester should avoid

Semester should not become:

- A page containing links to other university platforms
- An overcrowded dashboard
- A social-media platform
- A gamified student experience
- An unsourced AI chatbot
- A system that sends excessive notifications
- A tool that asks students to manually enter information already available
- A platform that combines official and estimated information without labels
- A replacement system without reliable migration and institutional controls

# Final positioning

> **Semester is the complete operating system for college—one secure application replacing disconnected learning platforms, email, calendars, registration, advising, financial accounts, study tools, and campus services.**

Its central advantage is not simply putting existing portals on one screen. Semester should **combine their information and workflows**, allowing students to search, plan, study, communicate, submit work, register, pay, and access campus life from one consistent application.
