## 1. What these cover

Semester communities are spaces inside your school: course and study groups,
student organizations, career and alumni groups, peer mentoring, support
circles, research groups, campus bulletins, events, and housing and
transport. Only people at your school can see them; there is no public feed.
These guidelines also apply to class rooms, group work and mentor requests.

## 2. Be someone people want to study with

**Not allowed:**
- Harassment or bullying, and threats or anything that makes someone fear for
  their safety.
- Hate or discrimination based on who someone is.
- Sharing private information about anyone (doxxing).
- Pretending to be someone else, including an instructor or an office.
- Sexual, violent or exploitative content, and any intimate image shared
  without the consent of the person in it.
- Spam, scams, phishing and malware.
- Answer keys, graded work, or offers to do someone's assessment for them, and
  anything a course's AI or collaboration rules forbid.

**In career, research, alumni and mentor spaces**, also: be accurate about
who you are and what you are offering; never pressure a student for contact,
financial, immigration, health or academic details; use structured requests
and honour a "no"; and do not use Semester to build contact lists for use
elsewhere.

## 3. Names

You post under your name. A school can approve a small number of support or
study communities where you may use an alias; there, other members see only
that name, but Semester still knows it is you, and trained trust & safety
staff can check during a safety investigation. Aliases cannot send direct
messages or post images, and post less often. **Alias posting is not switched
on anywhere today.**

## 4. Reporting

Use **Report** and choose what is wrong: harassment or bullying; a threat or
safety concern; hate or discrimination; private information or doxxing;
impersonation; non-consensual media; spam, scam or phishing; academic
integrity; or something else. Mark it urgent if someone may be in immediate
danger.

**Semester Community is not monitored as an emergency-response service.** If
you or someone else is in danger, contact emergency services or your campus
safety office now.

You can also **block** someone. Blocking takes effect immediately.

## 5. What happens after a report

- A person decides. Automated systems may only protect in the meantime — slow
  a post's reach, limit posting, or keep evidence — never remove content or
  restrict an account on their own.
- The most serious reports (immediate danger, doxxing, non-consensual images)
  go first, to trained staff.
- Possible outcomes: no action; a label; reduced reach; removal; locking a
  thread or limiting replies; a posting limit; a restriction in that
  community; or a restriction on the account.
- Where it is safe, we tell the person reported what was decided and why.
- **Appeal**: ask for a review, and a different reviewer decides.
- We do not promise a response time yet. [DECIDE: a target, once moderation
  is staffed.]

A school is told about a report only when it is among the most serious, a
written agreement with that school allows it, and two different reviewers
approve; this is switched off by default.

## 6. How long reports are kept

A report closed with no action is kept 90 days; one that led to action or an
appeal, one year; the audit record of moderation decisions, three years.
