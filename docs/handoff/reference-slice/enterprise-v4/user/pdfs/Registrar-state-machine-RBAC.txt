Below is the complete registrar workflow view tying together the state machine, RBAC by state, API surface, and
database schema for Semesterʼs scheduling and approval console. Institutions commonly use designated
department schedulers, chair/dean review, registrar coordination, and Academic Affairs or provost-level approval
for exceptions such as non-standard meeting times.
A realistic workflow separates standard approval from policy exceptions and central-room coordination. Registrar
workflow practices and scheduling policies commonly put departments and colleges on the build path, then route
special approvals through Academic Affairs or provost authority before final publication.

draft → validation_pending requires required fields, including term, course, section identity, capacity, and at
least one meeting proposal or explicitly approved TBA status.
validation_pending → department_ready requires no blocking conflicts and successful schedule-policy checks.
Standard meeting pattern enforcement is a common registrar rule because it reduces conflicts and improves
room use.
chair_review and dean_review confirm academic need, staffing feasibility, and college priorities before central
processing. Chair/dean review is explicitly part of published scheduling timelines at some institutions.
registrar_review → academic_affairs_review is required for non-standard scheduling, special approvals, or
policy exceptions. Some institutions reserve non-standard meeting-time approval to provost/Academic Affairs
authority.
room_coordination is required when central-room inventory is involved or when conflicts remain. Registrar
scheduling policies commonly centralize general-use classroom assignment.
approved_for_publish → published requires all approvals, resolved conflicts or logged waivers, and a
publishable snapshot.
published → change_requested is the controlled post-publication change path; institutions oen restrict who
can change published schedules and route those changes through workflow.
Education RBAC should distinguish view, edit, export, approval, and override authority, with access scoped to
legitimate institutional purpose and logged for audit.

Every approval, export, override, and role-sensitive action should emit an audit event with actor, scope, action, and
reason. Guidance on RBAC and FERPA-oriented systems emphasizes logging exports, overrides, and permission-
sensitive operations.
The workflow schema should separate academic data from approval workflow, conflict detection, and access
control. That keeps official schedule records stable while workflow actions remain append-only and auditable.

Three implementation principles matter most:
Keep workflow state on the section, but record every transition in append-only workflow tables.
Resolve permissions from role templates plus scope bindings, not hard-coded user flags.
Separate validation/conflict records from approvals so the system can distinguish technical failures from
human decisions.
That design matches how registrar and Academic Affairs workflows are commonly structured: departments build,
approvers route, registrars coordinate compliance and rooms, and special cases escalate to higher academic
authority before final publish.
