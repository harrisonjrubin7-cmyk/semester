Semester Institution OS should be the administrative brain of the platform: a unified operating
system for student engagement, opportunity operations, community governance, and
institutional intelligence. Modern education admin consoles center license or user
management, role-based permissions, analytics, and operational oversight; for Semester, that
same pattern expands to clubs, marketplace, study abroad, events, social feeds, trust and
safety, and campus-wide workflows.

A strong admin environment in education needs explicit role scoping, least privilege, logging,
and export controls. Role-based access design guidance for education emphasizes defining
what each role can view, edit, export, and administratively control, with audit logs for
permission-sensitive actions. FERPA guidance also requires institutions to use reasonable
methods so officials access only records in which they have a legitimate educational interest.
Semester Institution OS is not just a settings page. It should behave like a campus operating
layer with six major jobs:
Configure how Semester works at the institution level.
Run operational workflows across clubs, programs, listings, and feeds.
Govern who can access what data and why.
Surface real-time health and participation metrics.
Coordinate trust, privacy, and incident response.
Provide auditable evidence for leadership, compliance, and support teams.

A permission model should separate:
Permission type Example
View Read roster, application, event, or listing data
Edit Change records, publish content, update settings
Export Download CSV, reports, or evidence packs
Administer Assign roles, change policies, connect integrations
Override Reopen applications, remove content, bypass workflow rules
Education RBAC guidance recommends exactly this kind of concrete permission matrix and
logged override authority.

A global admin should not automatically see every sensitive field. Best practice articles on
FERPA compliance emphasize least privilege, periodic review, controlled sharing, and keeping
reporting inside governed systems rather than unmanaged exports.
Every sensitive action should log:
Who acted.
What record was accessed.
What action occurred.
When it happened.
Why it was allowed.
Which role and scope applied.
Whether data was exported or externally disclosed.
Education privacy and RBAC guidance consistently recommends logging authentication, role
changes, exports, overrides, and anomalous access.
Institution OS should support:
Recording student consents.
Tracking disclosures to third parties.

A practical role architecture could include:
Role Scope
Super admin Tenant-wide platform and security configuration
Student affairs admin Clubs, events, engagement, messaging
Global education admin Study-abroad programs and applications
Career services admin Marketplace, internships, employer verification
Trust & safety moderator Social reports, content actions, appeals
Compliance/privacy officer Audit, disclosures, consents, access reviews
Institutional analyst Aggregated analytics with restricted exports
Department operator Narrow scope by campus, college, or unit
Advisor/reviewer Assigned records only
Student leader admin Organization-only management with no institutional access
Each role should have a field-level and action-level permission matrix, not just page-level
access.

Auto-flag listings missing compensation or verification.

Detect clubs with leadership gaps before renewal season.
Predict event overcapacity and open overflow or waitlists.
