Below is a unified spec for Semesterʼs registrar approval matrix, workflow schema, transition APIs, immutable
audit model, and event-driven notification architecture. Modern academic scheduling systems are designed to let
departments build dras, route proposals and exceptions through workflow, validate against institutional rules,
and publish only aer central approval and coordination.
The permission model should enforce least privilege by role, scope, and action type, with separate rights for
viewing, editing, approving, exporting, and overriding. FERPA-oriented privacy guidance and education RBAC
guidance both emphasize granular access and comprehensive logging for sensitive records.

Scheduling platforms and registrar workflows commonly rely on rule-based validation and approval routing to
catch issues before publication.
The schema should separate academic data, workflow, RBAC, validation, notifications, and audit/FERPA into
distinct domains so approvals remain traceable and records stay governable.

Event-driven architecture fits institutional workflows because approvals, assignments, exceptions, and
publications are all discrete business events that should trigger notifications, integrations, and audit records
without tightly coupling every module. Higher-ed event-driven architecture guidance explicitly frames it as a way
to coordinate workflows, rules, and audit-preserving automation across departments and systems.

Which channels are allowed by event severity.

Examples:
Immediate in-app + email for workflow task assignment.
Escalation email if an approval task nears SLA breach.
No SMS for routine returns unless the institution enables it.
Special compliance routing for exports or FERPA disclosure events.

asynchronously. Event-driven architecture guidance for higher education highlights the need for defined
ownership, state preservation, policy control, and measurable outcomes across workflows.

The cleanest design is:
1. RBAC policy engine resolves action rights by role and scope.
2. Workflow service enforces state transitions and validation hooks.
3. Outbox/event bus emits domain events aer successful commits.
4. Notification engine handles fan-out, retries, and preferences.
5. Audit/FERPA service records immutable evidence for access, exports, and disclosures.

routing, final publish control, and a defensible compliance trail.
