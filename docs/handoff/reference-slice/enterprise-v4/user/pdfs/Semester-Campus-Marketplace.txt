Semester can extend beyond coursework into a verified campus opportunity marketplace: one
place for students to discover programs, jobs, services, events, and communities. Existing
student-engagement platforms commonly centralize organizations, events, opportunities,
communications, and participation records, which supports this direction.

Semesterʼs social layer should be a campus community product, not a copy of a broad public
social network. It should help students find people, activities, and support while providing
strong controls against harassment, misinformation, doxxing, and unwanted contact.
The home feed can combine:
Course-relevant announcements.
Club and event updates.
Internship and study-abroad deadlines.
Campus service reminders.
Followed topics and organizations.
Study-group requests.
Peer milestones, if the student elects to share them.
Curated institutional announcements.
“For you” opportunities based on explicit interests and enrolled-campus context.
Students should control:
Whether profile activity appears in feeds.
Who can comment, message, tag, or follow them.
Which clubs/topics can notify them.
Whether their attendance, club membership, or application activity is visible.
Feed personalization and sensitive-topic recommendations.
Chronological versus recommended feed views.
Academic grades, assignment submissions, accommodation information, counseling/wellness
activity, and private application details must never appear in a social feed.
Support:
Text, image, video, link, poll, and event posts.
Comments, reactions, saves, reposts, and topic tags.
Content warnings and alt text.
Accessibility-first captions and transcript support.
Post editing and deletion.
Student-controlled visibility: private, direct group, club, course cohort, campus, or public
external sharing where allowed.

Communities and group spaces
Messaging and networking
Semester should use a transparent ranking system:
1. Always prioritize urgent institutional safety messages.
2. Show followed organizations and explicit subscriptions.
3. Recommend opportunities from stated interests and saved searches.
4. Limit repetitive posts and low-quality engagement bait.
5. Explain recommendations: “Shown because you follow Environmental Science” or
“Matches your saved internship preferences.”
6. Offer “not interested,” mute, and reset-personalization controls.
7. Never use grades, disciplinary records, disability accommodations, health data, or private
messages for social recommendations.
Moderation tools need:
Clear community standards.
Reporting categories: harassment, threat, hate, impersonation, self-harm concern, scam,
privacy violation, sexual content, and misinformation.
Emergency escalation instructions.
Temporary content limits for repeated abuse.
Appeals and audit logs.
Moderator roles with least-privilege access.
Evidence retention schedules.
Human review for serious safety actions.
Institution-controlled policy configuration.
FERPA gives eligible students rights concerning education records and restricts certain
disclosures of personally identifiable information; this means Semester must carefully separate
social data from protected academic records and enforce institution-specific policies. Public
student content should not expose instructor feedback or grades.
