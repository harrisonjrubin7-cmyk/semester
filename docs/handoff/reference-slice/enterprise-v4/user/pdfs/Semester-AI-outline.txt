Semester AI is a student-focused Android app from Bawano Technologies that combines AI-
assisted PDF reading, document organization, course communities, and tutoring. Its Google
Play listing calls it “Semester AI – Reader, Tutors”; below is a breakdown of its publicly
documented capabilities—not a claim that I have tested every feature.
Semester AIʼs central feature is a PDF reader with an AI reading companion. The developer
describes the following functions.

A possible workflow is to scan class notes, review the resulting digital content, annotate it, and
share it with classmates. Those individual steps are supported by the listing, but automatic
execution of the entire workflow is not documented.
Semester AI also presents itself as a learning community organized around universities and
courses—not just a standalone chatbot.

The distinction matters: joining a course community is not the same as integrating with an
official university system. The listing does not establish enrollment verification, automatic
course imports, grade access, or connections to Canvas, Blackboard, Moodle, or other learning-
management systems.
The developer advertises an expanding catalog of course-specific AI and human tutors,
described as available for consultation and questions “24/7.” However, the listing does not
explain how that availability works or guarantee immediate access to a human tutor.

The description supports tutoring as a product category, but not detailed claims about adaptive
teaching, mastery tracking, automated grading, or personalized study plans.
The public description supports an AI-assisted study app. It does not establish an autonomous
agent that plans and executes multi-step tasks across external services. There is no documented
ability to send emails, manage your calendar, submit assignments, run code, operate a browser,
or connect to GitHub, Google Drive, Supabase, or Vercel. “Not documented” does not
necessarily mean absent—it means those abilities cannot be confirmed from this listing.
The search result preview mentioned quizzes, flashcards, and learning games, but the retrieved
full store description did not include those features. I would treat them as unconfirmed, rather
than promise they are currently available. Audio listening is likewise not explained in the
retrieved description.
Other details not established by the listing include:
The underlying AI model, context limits, and answer accuracy.
Offline AI operation and supported languages.
Pricing tiers, AI usage allowances, and storage limits.

The retrieved Google Play page shows 1K+ downloads, ads, in-app purchases, and a last-update
date of April 18, 2025. It does not provide a detailed subscription price list in the retrieved
content.
In its developer-provided data-safety disclosures, the app says it may collect location, personal
information, and seven other data categories; it declares no third-party data sharing,
encryption in transit, and the ability to request deletion. These are developer declarations, not
an independent privacy audit.
The two most distinctive documented capabilities are document-grounded reading help and
course-based collaboration: the first helps you work through your materials, while the second
connects those materials to classmates and tutors. Neither should be confused with a general-
purpose autonomous AI agent.
