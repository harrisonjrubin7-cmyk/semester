# 03 · Interview guides

Draft instruments. Consent wording and anything that touches legal meaning
need qualified counsel before use ([`08`](08-ETHICS-CONSENT-DATA-HANDLING.md)).

## How every session runs

60 minutes, one moderator and one note-taker who is not the moderator
(the founder should not be both; see friendly-participant bias in
[`01`](01-EVIDENCE-MODEL.md)).

| Min | Part | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Consent and set-up | Read the consent summary aloud; confirm each choice separately (participation, recording, internal quotes, external quotes, re-contact); say the participant may skip anything, stop at any time and keep the incentive. |
| 5–10 | Context | Role, tenure, a typical week. Establishes the segment tags; collects nothing identifying. |
| 10–40 | **The last time** | One recent, specific episode, told in order. Core of the session. |
| 40–50 | Artifacts and tour | Ask to be shown how they actually do it (calendar, notes, spreadsheet, a form). No account logins and no one else's information on screen. |
| 50–57 | Probes from the guide | The few topic questions below that the episode did not cover. |
| 57–60 | Close | "What should I have asked?" Re-state next steps and data handling. |

### Rules of questioning

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| "Tell me about the last time you…" | "Would you use…?" / "Wouldn't it help if…?" |
| "What did you do next?" "What happened then?" | Offer solutions or feature names before they raise a problem |
| "What do you use for that today?" (workarounds are the strongest need signal) | Ask for frequencies from memory ("how often do you…") — ask for the last three times instead |
| Silence; "say more about that" | Finish their sentences, nod at what you hope to hear |
| "What would you do if this were not available?" | Ask for price in the abstract (see willingness in [`04`](04-TESTING-AND-OBSERVATION.md)) |
| Record exact words in quotation marks and your own interpretation separately | Merge what was said with what you concluded |

Hypothetical and opinion answers are still recorded, tagged `opinion`, and
never counted toward C2.

## Guides by group

Each guide lists the core episode, then probes. "Hypothesis served" is the
register id each answer may inform ([`repository/register.json`](repository/register.json)).

### Students (first-year, transfer, graduate, international, commuter, working, first-generation)

*Core episode:* "Tell me about the last time something was due and you nearly
missed it, or did miss it."

- How did you first find out it was due? Where was that written down?
- Walk me through everything you use to keep track of school this week. Show me.
- When two sources disagreed about a date, what did you do?
- The last time you planned a week, who or what did you rely on?
- The last time you asked for help with school logistics — whom, how long did it take?
- Have you used an AI tool for coursework? Describe the last time, including what you did with the output. (Do not ask whether it is "cheating"; ask what the course policy said and how they found out.)
- What have you declined to put into an app, and why? (privacy and trust)
- Who else sees any of this? Anyone you would not want to?
- *Transfer/international/first-gen add:* Which part of how school works did you only learn by getting it wrong?
- *Working/commuter add:* When in the day does school planning happen?

### Accessibility users

*Core episode:* "Tell me about the last time a school tool didn't work with your setup."

- What do you use (screen reader, magnifier, switch, captioning, dictation, reading support)? Settings you rely on?
- Where in school work do you lose the most time to tools rather than content?
- Show me how you get through [an assigned core task] on your usual setup.
- What do you do when something is inaccessible — who do you ask, how long does it take?
- What have you stopped trying to use?
- Never ask about diagnoses or request documentation. If the participant volunteers, note the access need, not the condition.

### Faculty and TAs

*Core episode:* "Tell me about the last time you changed something mid-term and how students found out."

- Where does the course's single source of truth live? Who maintains it?
- How do students ask you about dates and policy? What gets asked repeatedly?
- Show me your syllabus. Which parts do students misread?
- What do you do about AI use in your course? What would make you trust or distrust a tool that shows students sources from your materials?
- What would you refuse to let a student tool do with your materials?

### Advisors

*Core episode:* "Walk me through your last advising meeting from the time it was booked."

- What did you know beforehand, from where? What did you have to ask?
- What do students bring that is useful? What is useless?
- What would you never rely on if the student typed it in?
- Caseload size, appointment length, no-show rate (ask for rough scale, flagged as `self-report`).
- What happens after: notes, follow-ups, referral. Who closes the loop?
- What are you prohibited from seeing or storing?

### Registrar

*Core episode:* "Tell me about the last correction or exception that came through your office."

- Who is the authority for each kind of record, and what is the correction path?
- Which third-party tools touch your data today, and how did each get approved?
- What would make you refuse a student-facing planning tool outright?
- What are your calendar crunch points (registration, drop/add, graduation certification)?
- What does "official" mean to you, in practice — what must be labelled, and how?
- Ask to see forms and policy pages (artifact review); do not ask to see records.

### Finance, bursar and aid

*Core episode:* "Tell me about the most common question that reaches your counter."

- Which questions can be answered without looking at an account? Which cannot?
- Where do students make avoidable mistakes (deadlines, forms, holds)?
- What is off limits to any third party, in your policy?
- What reconciliation failure would worry you most?
- (No individual accounts, amounts or aid details are discussed or shown.)

### Student affairs (residence life, basic needs, counselling liaison, conduct, activities)

*Core episode:* "Tell me about the last time a student was sent between offices."

- Where do students fall through? What does a good referral look like?
- What does a handoff require (capacity, wait, owner, escalation)? Use the eight blueprint questions in [`RESEARCH-AND-SERVICE-DESIGN.md`](../operating-model/RESEARCH-AND-SERVICE-DESIGN.md).
- What must never be automated or inferred about a student?
- What is your moderation and escalation duty, and who holds it?
- Crisis boundary: confirm with the participant what Semester must never attempt.

### IT, security and privacy

*Core episode:* "Tell me about the last third-party education tool you approved or rejected."

- What reviews did it pass through, and which one decided it? How long did it take?
- Show me the questionnaire you sent (HECVAT or local). Which answers decided it?
- Identity, provisioning and integration reality — what is actually deployed, versus the plan?
- What would you require before a student-chosen (not institution-bought) tool is allowed to connect?
- Who answers the phone when it breaks?

### Families

*Core episode (guardian):* "Tell me about the last time you needed to know something about your student's school situation."

- What did you learn, from whom? What did you want to know and couldn't?
- What do you believe you are entitled to see? Where does that belief come from?
- *Student, separately:* What do you share with your family now, and how? What would you not?
- Never relay one side's answers to the other. Never recruit a guardian through the student's account.

### Employers

*Core episode:* "Walk me through your last hiring cycle for an entry-level role."

- How did you find candidates, and what did you trust in a claim on an application?
- What do you pay for today (job boards, campus recruiting, assessments)?
- What would make a campus-issued verified credential matter to you? (Ask what they have *done* with ones they've seen.)
- What stops you recruiting at more campuses?

### Alumni

*Core episode:* "Tell me about the last time you used something from school after you left."

- What did you keep? What did you lose access to, and did it matter?
- How did you find your first role? Who helped?
- What would you want to keep from a student-era tool, and what would feel intrusive?

### Partners (providers, vendors, integrators)

*Core episode:* "Tell me about your most recent integration with a campus."

- What did the integration take (time, approvals, cost)? What failed?
- What are your terms, and which would you not bend on?
- What would you need from a platform like this to list or connect?
- What is your data-sharing restriction on students' information?

## After the session

1. Within 24 hours the note-taker files atomic observations (quote + context, no
   interpretation) in the restricted store ([`06`](06-RESEARCH-REPOSITORY.md)).
2. Within 48 hours the moderator and note-taker, independently, tag type and
   moment; disagreements are resolved by a recorded discussion, not deleted.
3. Only coarse segment tags enter the repository; recordings and raw notes stay
   in the restricted store under [`08`](08-ETHICS-CONSENT-DATA-HANDLING.md).

## Pre-test checks for any guide

Before a guide is used it must pass: every question is answerable from
experience; none names a Semester feature unprompted; each probe lists the
register id it informs; a pilot run with one non-participant confirms timing and
wording. A guide that fails is revised before it touches a participant.
