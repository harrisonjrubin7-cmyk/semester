## D-1059 · The degree audit is the school's, run on the server against the school's published program and its academic-record ledger, kept as it was run, and off until the school switches it to Core

**Decided 1 Oct 2026.** D-151 made the replacement of a school's systems a
module-at-a-time direction. `degree_audit` is on its list. Until now Semester's
only degree audit was `lib/degree.ts`, a calculator over requirements a student
types in, labelled as an estimate. That stays exactly as it is. This is the
other half: the school's own.

- **Two audits, kept apart on purpose.** The calculator is the student's
  arithmetic over what they typed. The new audit runs on the server over the
  school's published requirements and the school's ledger (D-145). Nothing in one
  reads or writes the other. The arithmetic is the same on purpose (double
  counting allowed and stated, in progress is never done, an empty `accepts`
  means anything, a bare uppercase prefix accepts every course with it).
- **Programs are immutable.** A school (`degree:author`: registrar, dean)
  makes a program for a catalog year as a draft, adds requirements, and
  publishes it. A published program never changes; a change is a new version,
  optionally copied from the last, and publishing it retires the one it
  replaces. A trigger refuses every other edit, the owner included.
- **Who audits.** `degree:audit` (registrar, dean, academic advisor) audits any
  student at their own school. Anybody else audits only the record the school
  linked to their own account. The date may not be later than tomorrow by the
  server's clock.
- **An audit writes nothing to the ledger.** It reads the entry in effect on the
  date for each key (a void means absent) and keeps one append-only row: who
  asked, the date, the program version, a SHA-256 and count of the lines read,
  and the result. Every mutation takes an idempotency key.
- **Nothing before the school says so.** Refused unless the school has switched
  `degree_audit` to Core, and while frozen or `kill.core_modules` is engaged.
  Every doubt reads Connect. No school has switched it.
- **One rule, two implementations.** The SQL function and its TypeScript twin
  must both reproduce one shared fixtures file exactly.

**The rules a school has to agree with, and has not been asked.** What counts as
passing is the program's own list, best first; none is defaulted. A course taken
twice counts twice. Transfer credit never meets a minimum grade, because it has
no grade. Hours come only from `credit` and transfer entries. Only enrollment,
grade, credit and transfer-credit entries are read, so a complete audit does not
mean a degree is conferred. These are the founder's reading of the common case,
not any registrar's. **Not decided:** the actual grade scales, repeat and
retake policy and transfer treatment of any school.

**Not done.** No what-if planner, transfer-evaluation workflow, catalogue import
or registrar sign-off. No authoring screen; programs are authored through the
database functions. An advisor reads every student's audit at the school, since
`degree:audit` has no narrower scope. No retention clock. Applied to no
database; **applying the migration needs the owner's decision.**
