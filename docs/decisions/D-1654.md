## D-1654 · A promoted school roster becomes course-and-term access, absence is not withdrawal unless the school says so, and roster scopes may be approved for outside connections

**Decided 2026-10-20.** The owner decided two things in chat: to start replacing a rostering service such as Clever alongside Slice 2, and to let outside applications receive roster data when the school approves the connection. The rest was decided by the assistant implementing those decisions, following the owner's uploaded Clever-to-Semester specifications; the founder can overrule any of it. Nothing here is applied to a hosted database.

**1. The roster is the source of course-and-term grants, not a second permission system.** `roster_project_grants` writes the same `role_grants` rows the gradebook and course tools already check, on scope `<school>/<CODE>/<TERM>`. Roster `student` becomes `student`, `teacher` becomes `faculty` (every co-teacher gets their own), and `aide` / `teaching_assistant` becomes `teaching_assistant`. Nothing else is assigned. The provenance is `institution`, and the existing trigger audits every change. The role launch register now names two writers of `role_grants`, the console and the roster, and its test asserts exactly those two.

**2. A batch says whether it is the whole roster.** `complete`, `partial` or `unknown`, set by the adapter, which says `partial` whenever it cannot prove it read every page. Partial and unknown batches merge and remove nothing. The five-argument `roster_stage` keeps its signature and means `complete`; the new behaviour is `roster_stage_batch`, a new name rather than an overload, because an overload with defaulted arguments would make every five-argument call ambiguous.

**3. Absence ends access only when the school has approved that reading.** `roster_import_config.absence_revokes_access`, off by default. Even when it is on, the live roster must rest on a complete batch with no partial batch after it. Two sources informed this: Clever documents that a deletion can mean "no longer shared with this application" rather than "withdrew", and the owner's migration plan sets `absence_means_removal: false` as the default. An explicit `inactive` or `tobedeleted` status is a statement, not an absence, and ends the roster's grant in any batch.

**4. The roster takes back only what it gave.** `private.roster_grant` records whether the roster created or re-activated a grant (`owned`) or found it already live from another source. Only owned grants are ever revoked by the roster. One consequence, accepted: if an administrator revokes a grant the roster still lists as active, the next projection re-activates it, because the roster is the institution's word on enrollment. Ending that access belongs in the source (the registrar), not in a manual revocation.

**5. Identity is an explicit binding, never email.** `private.roster_identity` maps a roster `sourcedId` to one account at the same school, one-to-one, by `scim`, `sso_subject` or `admin_review`. The roster rows never carry an email; the staging allowlist excludes it. Unbound people get nothing and are listed by `roster_unbound` for review.

**6. A batch read before the live roster is refused.** It is compared on `observed_at` against the newest promoted batch, so a delayed or replayed export cannot overwrite a newer one.

**7. Roster scopes may be approved for outside connections.** `roster` leaves `integration_scopes`' never-list. A roster scope must be `read` or `event` and must declare the education-record ceiling `T3`, so nobody grants one at the default `T0` by accident. Approval stays `integration_approve_scope`'s, which refuses the proposer. Grades, submissions, accommodations, health, counselling, conduct, instructor notes and aid stay forbidden.

**Not done.** No live source connector (a Clever connector also needs a Clever partner agreement). No admin screen for bindings, unbound people or the absence setting. No OIDC sign-in. No provisioning into outside applications. Who in a school may bind identities and turn on absence-means-removal is the school's decision and is not modelled beyond the service key. The database suites ran on PostgreSQL 16; production runs 17.
