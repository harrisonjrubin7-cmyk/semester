## D-1659 · A school administrator binds roster people and decides absence-means-removal, under one capability, never for their own account

**Decided 2026-10-20.** Built on D-1654, which left identity binding, the unbound list and the absence switch to the service key. The assistant that implemented this chose the defaults below. They are defaults, and the owner and each school can overrule any of them. Nothing here is applied to a hosted database.

**1. One capability, `roster:administer`, held over the school.** `private.has_capability` matches scope exactly, so a grant at another school, or over a course or office, does not reach the desk. The capability goes to `integration_admin`, who configures a school's connections (and the roster import is one of them), and to `university_admin`, who approves them. Together they are the roles that already administer a school's integrations (`integration:configure`, `integration:approve`). It is not given to a registrar, faculty, a student or any platform role; a platform operator reaches a school only through a break-glass grant that names the capability.

**2. Never your own account, and only somebody on the roster.** A binding gives the bound account the roster's course grants, including `faculty`. So the desk refuses to let an administrator bind their own account, and it refuses a sourcedId that is not a user in the live roster. The other refusals come from `roster_bind_identity`: an account at another school, a second account for one person, and a second person for one account.

**3. Idempotent, optimistic and audited.** Writes are keyed in `private.roster_admin_operation`, which has the gradebook's ledger shape: a repeat returns the first answer and writes nothing, and refusals are not stored. The absence switch carries the value the administrator saw; if it has changed since, nothing is written. Turning the switch on re-projects at once. The read reports beforehand how many roster-given grants would end, and the receipt reports how many did. Each write is a pseudonymous `public.audit_event` row (`roster.identity_bound`, `roster.absence_setting_changed`).

**4. No domain event.** `events.ts` admits a new name only together with its first consumer, and nothing downstream acts on a binding except the projection, which runs in the same transaction.

**5. Unbind ends the access the binding produced, in the same transaction.** (`20261021100000_roster_admin_unbind_search.sql`.) #1654's projection does not answer what happens when a binding disappears: it revokes a roster-owned grant only on an explicit inactive status, or on absence with the school's switch on, and a person whose binding is gone is neither. So the conservative answer was chosen. `roster_admin_unbind` deletes the binding and revokes, with `revoked_at` as the projection does, every course grant the roster *owns* for that account at that school. A grant the roster only noted (an administrator, an LTI launch or SCIM gave it first) is kept, as on every other roster path. Nothing is silently left live, and a later promotion does not bring the access back. The `roster_grant` rows stay, so binding the person to the same account again re-activates the same grant rows through the projection's own branch. The other rules mirror binding: `roster:administer` over that school; never your own binding (a colleague can remove it); idempotent in the same ledger (a third kind, `unbind`); audited as `roster.identity_unbound` (pseudonymous, with the binding's method and how many grants ended). It is optimistic: the call carries the account the administrator saw bound. If the person is now bound to another account, nothing is written (`stale`). If nobody is bound any more, the answer is `already-unbound`. A binding made by SCIM or an SSO subject can be removed too, and the next SCIM push may bind it again.

**6. People search is the school's unbound accounts, minimally.** `roster_admin_find_accounts` answers only accounts whose profile is at the caller's school. That is the population `roster_bind_identity` accepts. It never returns an account already bound there, or the caller's own (neither could be bound). It matches the account's chosen handle or its sign-in address as a literal, case-insensitive fragment of at least 3 characters (`%` and `_` match themselves). It returns at most 20 rows, each with the account id the binding needs, the handle, and the address masked to its first character and domain (`m•••@school.edu`). It never returns the full address, and never a name the account did not choose. The paste-an-id field is gone; nothing needed it once search existed. `roster_admin_bound` lists who is bound (at most 1000), including people who have left the roster, so they can still be unbound.

**7. No new capability and no new event.** Unbind and search use `roster:administer` and the audit trail; nothing downstream consumes a binding change except the projection, which runs in the same transaction (the reasoning of 4).

**For the owner.** (a) Whether the capability should sit with these two roles, with fewer, or also with the registrar. (b) Whether binding, or turning absence-means-removal on, should need a second person. (c) Whether a second person should be required for faculty bindings, since an administrator can bind any account at the school other than their own, including one they also control. (d) Whether turning the switch on should act immediately (as built) or only from the next roster. (e) Whether an unbind should end roster-given access at once (as built) or keep it until the next roster. (f) Whether a SCIM- or SSO-made binding should be removable at the desk, or only by the source that made it. (g) Whether the search should also match the sign-in address (as built) or the handle alone, and whether the masked address is more than an administrator needs.

**Not done.** A domain event. A visual check in a running app. A two-person rule for any of these writes (owner question b). The database suites ran on PostgreSQL 16; production runs 17.
