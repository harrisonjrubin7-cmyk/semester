## D-1184 · A later order form can raise a school's plan, never lower it

**Decided 2026-10-04.** `private.apply_signed_contract` no longer lets the last order form signed win. Signing an order form whose tier is lower than a school's current plan, or whose `ends_at` is earlier than that plan's, is refused with a check violation (`23514`); the signing fails as a whole and the contract stays unsigned. Same tier on the same dates, a higher tier, a longer term and a renewal are applied. A plan that is `ended`, or whose `ends_at` has passed, is replaced freely.

**Why.** The function compared only against the incoming values. A campus order form ending 2027-06-30 followed by a department pilot ending 2026-12-31 replaced the campus tier and end date with the pilot's. The change was recorded in `tenant_plan_history`, so it was recoverable, but nothing prevented it, and no check covered overlapping order forms (finding 6 and §12 step 1 of `docs/commercial/REVENUE-OPERATIONS-ARCHITECTURE.md`; `tenant_plan` is read by the entitlement order's tenant-plan step, `docs/ENTITLEMENT-RESOLUTION.md`, so a quietly shortened plan is a refusal at launch for a school that paid for more).

**The rule's edges.** A current plan is one that is not `ended` and has no `ends_at` in the past. A dated order against an open-ended plan counts as shorter; an open-ended order is never shorter. A higher tier with a shorter end is also refused: it is one order doing two things, and the shorter end is the part the school's plan would lose. A contract already applied to a school is skipped for it, found by its history row `Order form <id> signed.`, so replaying an older order form, or the backfill, over a later upgrade is a no-op rather than a downgrade or a refusal.

**Refuse, not amend.** The preferred rule was to refuse or require an `amendment`. This refuses only. An `amendment` still triggers nothing, and a deliberate downgrade or shortening is the service role writing `tenant_plan` itself, which leaves its own history row and reason. Making an amendment able to lower a plan needs a decision about who approves one and what it supersedes, which this does not make.

**Not done.** `apply_signed_contract` still sets `status = 'active'` on an applied order, so signing a higher tier over a `suspended` plan lifts the suspension; a suspended plan counts as current here and is protected from lowering, but its status is not. Nothing marks the earlier contract `superseded` when an upgrade lands. Checked on Postgres 16 locally; the repository pins 17, so CI is the authority.
