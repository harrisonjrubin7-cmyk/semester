## D-1479 · The saga runner's `Store` over Postgres is a translator, not a second judge

**Decided 11 Oct 2026**, continuing C-6 on the owner's instruction. Follows [D-1361](D-1361.md); the functions it calls are that decision's, with [#1363](https://github.com/harrisonjrubin7-cmyk/semester/pull/1363)'s two fixes.

- **What was decided.** `PostgresAccessSagaStore` (`app/server/institution/postgres-access-saga.ts`) implements `Store` by calling `access_saga_claim`, `access_saga_save`, `access_saga_flag_recovery`, `access_saga_release` and `access_saga_wake` through the service client, and does nothing else. It lives next to the other service-only adapters (`postgres-journal.ts`), not in `packages/institution`, which has no database client and must stay importable by the gateway without one.
- **The lease, the revision and the transition are decided in the database.** The adapter re-checks none of them. An adapter that re-checked could disagree with the function that wrote the row, and the disagreement would be a bug nobody could see from either side.
- **One error is the saga's; every other is the store's.** SQLSTATE `SC409` means another owner or a cancellation moved the saga, and becomes `PersistenceConflict`, which the runner answers by standing down. Everything else, `SC404` and an outage alike, is a storage failure, so the runner never mistakes it for a participant's fault and never retries a step on its account.
- **A row the runner could misread is not a lease.** `claim` reads the returned row into the record field by field and fails closed on a state outside the fifteen, an action nobody decides, an unsafe integer, a missing cleanup flag or another saga's id. The alternative, trusting the row's shape, would let a schema drift become a runner decision.
- **`create` is outside `Store`.** The runner only meets sagas that exist. Insertion goes through the table's own checks, at revision 0, and a refusal there is a storage error.
- **Held by.** Fifteen tests over a scripted service client, with controls. Two mutations run and both went red: `SC408` for `SC409` fails the two conflict tests; dropping the claimed-id check fails the malformed-row test.
- **Not done.** No worker runs the runner over this store; the revocation barrier at a real enforcement point; the crash rehearsals through the adapter against a live database (local Postgres 16 here, 17 in CI).
