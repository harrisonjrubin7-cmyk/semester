## D-1047 · Assignments and submissions are the first Core module of the learning half: a receipt the database writes, per-student extensions with a reason, and nothing before the school switches them on

**Decided 1 Oct 2026.** D-151 made the replacement of a school's systems a
module-at-a-time direction, and D-152 built the switch. This is the first
module behind it that a student and an instructor can use: `lms_assignments`.

- **Who does what.** A faculty member with `assignments:author` on a course
  and term writes a draft, revises it while it is a draft, publishes it, and
  closes it. A teaching assistant holds `assignments:review`, reads every
  submission and is offered nothing that writes. A student is whoever the
  gradebook already calls on the roster (`grades:receive`); there is one list of
  who is in a course and this does not keep a second. Every grant is on
  `<school>/<CODE>/<TERM>`, so a grant with no term authorises nothing and this
  spring's instructor reads nothing of last year's section.
- **Nothing before the school says so.** Every write is refused unless the
  school has switched `lms_assignments` to Core, and while the module is frozen
  or `kill.core_modules` is engaged. Reading is not gated by mode: what was
  submitted stays readable. The screen reads the same mode the database does and
  every doubt reads Connect, so a read that failed never shows the module.
- **What a receipt is.** The database writes it in the same transaction as the
  version it names: a code, the SHA-256 of the text, the time it took it, the
  due time that applied to that student then, and whether it was late. The
  student can check it against their own text on their own device. It proves what
  Semester took and when. It is not a grade and not the school's record.
- **Nothing is overwritten, nothing is doubled.** A submission is a container
  and every version is a new row. Every write takes an idempotency key: the same
  key and request answers the same and writes nothing, and the same key for
  anything else is refused. A copy of the latest version is refused, so is a
  version past the cap and a second where resubmission is off. The screen keeps
  one key across a lost reply and takes a new one after an answer.
- **Late is the instructor's call, in the data.** Work after the due time is
  taken and marked late until `closes_at` when one is set, or until the
  instructor closes it. An extension moves one student's due time, later only,
  with a reason, and is a row; the newest is in force. An extension never
  leaves a student closed before their own due time, and it does not reopen a
  closed assignment.
- **Drafts stay on the device until submitted.** The text box autosaves with
  `useDraft`, comes back after leaving, and clears only once a receipt is in.
  A submission is refused offline rather than held: one that fires hours later
  is a surprise (D-055, D-155).

**What this did not do.** Text only: no file, link, media or group submission.
Attached files need somewhere to live and whether they upload over a phone
plan is an owner decision, as it is for file sync. No grading: the gradebook
of record is separate and an assignment does not yet feed it. The roster is not
readable by a client, so an extension goes to a student who has submitted or by
account id, as the gradebook does. Not applied to production; applying the
migration needs owner approval. No school has switched the module, and the
register says `In preparation` and no more.

**How it is held.** A 114-check SQL suite across two schools (who authors, who
submits, who reads, late, extensions, close, Connect, frozen, paused, and that
an account that has submitted is not "untouched"); five deliberate breaks of the
migration each turn it red. TypeScript: 83 tests, six deliberate breaks each
red. Where the client and the migration can drift, a test reads the migration
and holds the limits, states and argument names equal.
