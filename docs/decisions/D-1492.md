## D-1492 · The worker's tick asks the database what is due and adds no rule of its own

**Decided 12 Oct 2026.** The access saga runner (D-1361) had a Store on Postgres (D-1479) and crash rehearsals (D-1481) but nothing that chose which sagas to tick. Something had to, and the question was where the choosing lives.

- **What was decided.** The choosing is a database function, `access_saga_due`, and it mirrors what the runner does with each state on a tick: a working state is due once its next attempt has passed, a waiting state only once its provisioning deadline has, `ACTIVE` only once its grant has expired, a finished state never, and nothing under a live lease. The tick (`runAccessSagaTick`) runs the runner once per listed saga within a cap and a budget and decides nothing else: what a move may do is the runner's and the store's, and a storage failure on one saga is counted and does not stop the rest.
- **Why the database and not the worker.** The worker would have to read every saga to decide, and two workers would read the same rows; the function reads once, in the index the store already has on `next_attempt_at`, and the claim behind it is what makes two workers safe. The list is advice; the claim is the decision.
- **Why the rule follows the runner's states and not `next_attempt_at` alone.** The first version listed every unfinished saga past its next attempt. The rehearsal that drives a saga to `ACTIVE` then found it due on every tick after, and the store check listed a wait within its deadline and a live grant. Both are sagas the runner would claim, read, and release unmoved, so a tick over them is a lease taken for nothing and a list that never empties. The rule now says what the runner says: only a clock the runner acts on makes a saga due.
- **Why two overlapping ticks may move one saga twice.** A saga one tick moved is due again when the other lists it, and the second tick moves it a further step. That is two moves of one revision each, with one event per revision and no lease left, and the rehearsal holds it to exactly that. Preventing it would need the list and the claim to be one transaction, which would hold a lock across the runner's participant calls.
- **Held by.** `access-saga-store.check.sql` at 160 checks; the due rule reduced to `next_attempt_at` alone fails it ("expected 5, got 7"). Four rehearsals in `postgres-access-saga.integration.test.ts` against the migrated database; the same reduction fails the `ACTIVE` one first.
- **Not done.** Nothing schedules the tick in production: no Edge Function and no `scheduler.sql` job, which is the shape `integration-tick` has and the next step. No real `Services` exist to hand it, so the runner still runs only in tests. The revocation barrier at a real enforcement point stays open.
