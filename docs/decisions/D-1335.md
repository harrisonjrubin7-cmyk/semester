## D-1335 · Device permissions are a panel on Your data that reads and never asks, not a settings page

**Decided 2026-10-06**, by the assistant under the executive authority the founder delegated in-session (D-1287). The founder can overrule it.

**Context.** Stream 02 asks for a Capabilities and Permissions screen covering camera, microphone, location and notifications, each with a fallback and a revoke path. The pieces exist, scattered: the barcode scanner, recording and dictation, the saved-places reader, and the push switch. No screen shows all four.

**Decisions.**
1. It is a panel, `DevicePermissions`, on the Your data page, not a page of its own. The settings index is capped at eleven rows so it fits a phone without scrolling (`lib/settings.test.ts`), and that test says a twelfth should merge two rows rather than raise the cap. Merging two unrelated settings pages is a separate design change, so the cap stands. Your data is already where "what leaves the device" lives, and settings search finds the panel through that row.
2. The panel reads and never asks. Each feature asks for its own permission when the student starts the job that needs it. A test fails if reading prompts for anything.
3. Revoking camera, microphone and location is the browser's, not the page's. The panel gives directions to the browser's site settings and does not draw a switch that would do nothing. Notifications are the app's own subscription, so they link to the existing switch on the Alerts page rather than adding a second one.
4. A browser that will not report camera or microphone until asked is "Not reported", not "Not asked yet". A failure to read at all offers a retry and shows no state it made up.

**Consequences.** One place answers "what can this app use, and how do I stop it". The state words live in `lib/permissions.ts` and not `lib/status.ts`, whose vocabulary is fixed to provenance and sync; folding them in is a design-system decision left open.
