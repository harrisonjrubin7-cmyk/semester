## D-1042 · The business case starts from a stack-consolidation calculator that counts a system as removed only when its contract has ended and the replacement is available, and takes Semester's price from the school

**Decided 30 Sep 2026** (S3 of the site brief). D-141 and D-151 made the
replacement of a school's systems, one module at a time, the direction; a buyer's first question is what it does to
their software bills. The calculator answers it without saying more than is
true.

- **Where.** The company site's business-case page opens on it, beside the
  advising, registration and AI-readiness calculators; `/tools/stack/` is the
  same calculator on the prerendered site. One module, `lib/stackcost.mjs`,
  holds the arithmetic; the company site carries a copy between two markers
  that `npm run registers` rewrites, and `stackcost.test.ts` runs that copy
  against the module. The fourteen rows come from `site/modules.ts`, the same
  list the replacement map prints, so the calculator cannot name a module the
  map does not.
- **A system is removed only when both are true.** Its contract has ended and
  Semester's replacement is available: the year of the switch is the later of
  the year after the contract ends and the year the school enters for the
  replacement. The register's word for each module is printed beside it, and
  today it is *Planned* for ten of the fourteen and *In preparation* for the
  other four; none is *Available now*. The calculator does not read that word
  to move a date, because none exists; it says the year is the school's
  assumption, not a Semester commitment.
- **Semester's price is the school's, from a quote.** Semester has no published
  institutional price (D-134 and the pricing page say so), so nothing is filled
  in: a row with no price is never switched off and saves nothing. Illustrative
  numbers exist behind one button, labelled as placeholders, the same shape
  for every row, and dropped the moment the visitor types.
- **What it shows.** Five years, year by year: the current stack (with a yearly
  increase the school sets), with Semester, the saving and its running total;
  payback by month; admin hours freed (the school's own figure); and a timeline
  of which system is switched off in which year, or why not. The method, every
  assumption and the limits are on the page. It downloads as CSV and prints.
  "Email me this" on the company site goes through the existing general-contact
  route, and sends nothing without a name and an address (otherwise the visitor's
  own mail app is opened). The tool page sends nothing at all.
- **Held by tests.** Two fixtures worked by hand (750,000 today, 540,000 with
  Semester, 210,000 saved, payback in month 13; and a deficit year, payback in
  month 18); a switch with no price, no year, no cost, or after year five is
  refused, each shown red when its rule is broken; the tool page's figures equal
  the module's; and the register's word for every module is printed.
- **Not decided here.** Semester's institutional price, and any date for a
  Core module. Until they exist this is a calculator of the school's own
  numbers, and it says so.
