## D-1504 · The access saga's participants live in Postgres, and the revocation barrier is a row the install checks

**Decided 12 Oct 2026.** The runner states six duties that only a real administrator and enforcement point can keep (D-1338, D-1361, D-1492, D-1500). Until one exists, the registry holds `null` and the scheduled tick counts sagas without moving them. The question was where the two participants live, and where the barrier that makes revocation hold is enforced.

- **What was decided.** The policy administrator and the enforcement point are Postgres objects, in `20261012050000_access_participants.sql`, behind six `security definer` functions executable by the service role alone. `PostgresAccessParticipants` maps them onto the runner's `Services`. The registry still holds `null`, because the policy decision point (`validate`, `evaluate`) does not exist yet.
- **Why in the database and not in a remote system.** The runner's hardest claim is that a late install cannot undo a revocation. In a database that claim is one row, `access_revocations`, which only rises, read in the same transaction as the install. A remote administrator would make it a promise about someone else's uptime and ordering. Here the rehearsal can show it: a saga revoked at INSTALLING, then the install's late message under its original key, refused with nothing held.
- **Why the barrier only rises, enforced by a trigger.** A lowered barrier would reopen every generation it had closed. The trigger refuses it, and the check proves the refusal, so a future migration that lowers it by hand fails the same test.
- **Why a receipt for each successful operation and not for each refusal.** A repeated call under the same key must return what the first returned, so a timed-out prepare or install can be retried safely. A refusal is not recorded because it repeats on its own: a barrier only rises and an expiry only passes, so asking again gives the same answer.
- **Why a refusal is a named fault and everything else is the participant unavailable.** The runner's decisions depend on what a failure means. A superseded or revoked grant is not retried, a stale decision is read as a denial in PREPARING, and an outage of a write is an outcome unknown that must be inspected before anything is assumed. The mapping keeps those three apart.
- **Held by.** `access-participants.check.sql` at 133 checks, including the barrier first and a mutation that removes the install's barrier check and goes red; `postgres-access-participants.test.ts` at 10; four adapter rehearsals through the runner on a migrated database, 36 of 36 in the file.
- **Not done.** The policy decision point, so the registry still holds `null`. The job stays parked until the function's first deployment answers its test request. The four tables have no retention clock, as their saga siblings do not.
