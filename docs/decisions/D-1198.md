## D-1198 · Erasure clears the erased account's id from the policy audit's JSON snapshots

**Decided 4 Oct 2026** by the owner, from two options (scrub, or keep and document the exception) (migration `20261004190000_erasure_scrubs_audit_copies.sql`; found in [`../privacy-operations/08-FINDINGS-AND-RECONCILIATION.md`](../privacy-operations/08-FINDINGS-AND-RECONCILIATION.md) C5).
`audit_tenant_policy_change` copies every change to `consent_record` into `tenant_policy_audit_event.old_data` / `new_data` as `to_jsonb(old)` / `to_jsonb(new)`, which carries `subject_user_id` and `recorded_by`. `erase_account` finds what to remove from `private.account_data_map()`, which sees foreign-key columns only, so those copies outlived the account. A check written from reading the SQL (`erasure-clears-consent-snapshots.check.sql`) failed with "expected 0, got 4" on current code, locally and in CI on PostgreSQL 17.

- **What was decided.** `public.erase_account` (the hold-checking wrapper) now runs `private.scrub_audit_snapshots(target)` after `private.erase_account_unheld`. It replaces the erased id with the nil UUID wherever it appears in either JSON column. The audit rows stay, with their capability, status, policy version and timestamps, and other people's ids in the same rows are untouched. The scrub function is reachable only through the wrapper, and the receipt gains an `audit_snapshots_scrubbed` count.
- **Why after, not before.** Deleting the account cascades to its consent rows and the audit trigger writes a `delete` snapshot for each. A scrub run first leaves exactly those (watched: "expected 0, got 2").
- **What it costs.** It edits a log that is otherwise append-only; nothing else in the table is protected by an immutability trigger, and the retention rule for it is "goes with the school". The schools' audit therefore shows that a consent change happened, not whose.
- **Held by.** The database check (6 checks, with controls: the copies exist before, the other person's copies survive), watched red before the migration ("expected 0, got 4") and green after; and a test in `erasure.test.ts` that fails if the wrapper loses the hold check, the erasure, or the scrub, or runs them out of order (watched red: "expected 441 to be less than 389").

**Not decided here.** Whether this is what the law requires, or whether a school's audit may keep such copies, is counsel's (P-08 in [`../privacy-operations/07-COUNSEL-REVIEW-QUEUE.md`](../privacy-operations/07-COUNSEL-REVIEW-QUEUE.md)). The decision can be reversed by removing the scrub call from the wrapper and changing the check. The unsalted audit pseudonyms (08 C8) are a separate finding, untouched.
