## D-1645 · A hand-off is guessed at five times, then it is revoked, and the service role can revoke one that is unused

**Decided 18 October 2026.**

`consume_handoff` answers null to a wrong id, a wrong nonce, a used hand-off and an expired one, so its answer tells a guesser nothing. What limited guessing was only the nonce's length (16 or more characters) over the fifteen minutes a hand-off lives. The table's `revoked` status existed and no function set it, so a link sent to the wrong person could not be cancelled.

Decided: a wrong nonce against a hand-off that is still live counts in `attempts`; the fifth sets it `revoked`, and the right nonce is refused from then on. A made-up id, a nonce too short to be real, and a used or expired hand-off count against nothing, so a stranger cannot spend someone else's hand-off without holding its id. `public.revoke_handoff(uuid)`, service role only, revokes an unused hand-off and answers whether it did. Five, because a person who mistypes a code once or twice still gets in, and a guesser gets five tries out of 2^64 or more.

Evidence: `supabase/onboarding-journeys.check.sql` failed first on the missing column, then passes at 66 checks, including four wrong nonces leaving the hand-off usable, the fifth revoking it, the right nonce refused after, short nonces counting nothing, the revoker's three outcomes, and the grant list. Whole-head gates are in PR 1645.

Not done: `20261012110000_handoff_attempt_limit_and_revoker.sql` is not applied to production (a separate approved step). Nothing calls `create_handoff`, `consume_handoff` or the new revoker yet, and there is no `/open/<route>` resolver, so `P5-N28` stays `partial`. The two-session case (two wrong nonces sent at once against the fourth attempt) is not driven; the count is a single update per call and cannot go past five, which the column's check enforces.
