The vision now covers most student-facing activities. What is still missing is the infrastructure, institutional operations, trust systems, and focused rollout strategy needed to make Semester a realistic replacement rather than an extremely large list of features.

# Most important missing elements

## 1. A clearly defined first customer

Semester currently appears designed for everyone:

- Students
- Universities
- Professors
- Advisors
- Athletes
- Clubs
- Employers
- Alumni

You need to decide who adopts and pays for it first.

The strongest path would likely be:

- **Primary user:** college students
- **Initial buyer:** university or individual student
- **Initial problem:** disconnected coursework and studying
- **Initial product:** unified courses, syllabus processing, calendar, assignments, and adaptive studying

Without this focus, Semester could become too large to build, explain, sell, and test.

## 2. A realistic minimum viable product

The full vision includes replacements for dozens of mature products. The first version should prove one connected workflow exceptionally well.

A strong MVP would include:

1. Account and university connection
2. Course dashboard
3. Syllabus upload
4. Automatic deadline extraction
5. Unified assignments and calendar
6. Course-material upload
7. Eleven study formats
8. Source-grounded AI tutor
9. Personalized study planner
10. Practice quizzes and progress tracking

Documents, presentations, email, video editing, registration, payments, and campus operations can follow later.

## 3. Student-information system

Semester needs a central institutional record system containing:

- Student identity
- University identification number
- Enrollment
- Courses
- Credits
- Grades
- Academic standing
- Major and minor
- Transfer credits
- Graduation status
- Holds
- Transcript
- Enrollment verification

This is the foundation behind registration, degree planning, advising, and official grades.

## 4. Admissions and new-student onboarding

Semester should support the student before classes begin.

Missing capabilities include:

- University applications
- Application status
- Admission decisions
- Deposit payments
- Financial-aid offers
- Orientation
- Placement tests
- Housing applications
- Student ID setup
- Course-placement information
- Required forms
- Transcript submission
- AP, IB, and transfer-credit evaluation
- New-student checklists

## 5. Official transcripts and academic records

- View unofficial transcripts
- Order official transcripts

- Verify enrollment
- Request degree verification
- View transfer-credit decisions
- Access tax and educational documents
- Track transcript requests
- Share verified credentials

- Billing
- Financial aid
- Institutional reporting
- Policy management
- Audit logs
- Data retention
- Emergency communications

Semester needs role-specific dashboards instead of one general administrative interface.

Universities depend on multi-step approvals. Semester needs a configurable workflow system for:

- Major changes

Add:

- Library-catalog search
- Academic-database search

- Citation management
- Source storage
- PDF annotation
- Literature-review organization
- Research-project workspaces
- Data collection

- Research-advisor collaboration
- Source credibility evaluation

## 10. Writing and originality support

Documents need:

- Citation verification
- Accidental-plagiarism warnings
- Quotation checking
- Source attribution
- Version history
- Authorship history
- Rubric-based review
- Grammar and clarity feedback
- Discipline-specific writing guidance

AI should never invent quotations, citations, facts, or student experiences.

## 11. Academic-integrity framework

Because Semester contains powerful AI, institutions need detailed controls.

Professors should be able to define:

- Whether AI is allowed
- Permitted AI functions
- Whether solutions can be shown
- Collaboration rules
- Citation requirements
- Assessment restrictions
- Disclosure requirements

Semester should record whether content is:

- Student-created
- AI-assisted
- Collaboratively edited
- Directly quoted
- Instructor-provided

This should support transparency without automatically accusing students.

## 12. AI reliability and evaluation

A statement that AI is source-grounded is not enough. Semester needs systems for:

- Citation validation
- Hallucination detection
- Source-quality ranking
- Conflicting-source detection
- Confidence reporting
- Human correction
- Student error reporting
- Model-performance testing
- Bias testing
- Course-specific accuracy checks
- Prompt-injection protection in uploaded files

Important dates, grades, bills, policies, and registration actions should never rely solely on an AI interpretation.

## 13. Accessibility and accommodations center

Capabilities should include:

- Accommodation letters
- Extended assessment time
- Reduced-distraction testing
- Accessible course materials
- Note-taking support
- Captioning
- Alternative document formats
- Interpreter scheduling
- Accessible transportation
- Faculty notifications
- Privacy-controlled accommodation records

Possible signals include:

- Repeated missing assignments

## 25. Global notification logic

Semester needs more than notification settings. It needs a priority engine.

Notifications should be classified as:

- Emergency
- Action required
- Deadline approaching
- Informational
- Promotional

The system should:

- Remove duplicates
- Combine related notifications
- Respect quiet hours
- Escalate uncompleted critical actions
- Provide daily summaries
- Explain why a notification was sent

Students should retain access to:

- Class schedule
- Student ID where technically possible
- Downloaded course files
- Notes
- Study guides
- Flashcards
- Assignments
- Travel details
- Emergency information

Users and institutions need to know:

- Who owns AI-generated materials

- When data is deleted
- What happens after graduation

Semester should support standard exports rather than trapping institutions or students inside the platform.

# Biggest strategic gap: scope control

The largest missing component is not another feature. It is a strict distinction between:

- What Semester must build first
- What it should build later
- What it must support institutionally
- What can remain a specialized extension

# The single most important missing experience

Semester needs a connected **daily action system** that turns everything into one manageable plan:

1. Semester collects course and campus information.
2. It identifies what requires action.
3. It ranks tasks by urgency and importance.
4. It builds a realistic daily plan.
5. It opens the correct study or work tool.
6. It tracks completion and understanding.
7. It automatically adjusts the remaining schedule.

That would connect the enormous feature set into one coherent product.

Semester should ultimately promise:

> **Semester does not simply contain every school tool. It understands how classes, studying, assignments, schedules, communication, campus life, and career preparation connect—and turns them into one clear daily experience.**
