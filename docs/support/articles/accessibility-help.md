# Accessibility help

> **Type:** help · **Audience:** students, support · **Owner:** `accessibility` · **Truth:** held · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-04 · **Held by:** `app/src/lib/docs/support.test.ts`

Use this page when something in Semester is hard to read, hard to operate or blocks you, and you want the settings that help and the way to report a barrier; stop reading if you are looking for a conformance statement, because none exists.

**Status:** PARTIAL. Automated accessibility checks run on every build and find a minority of barriers. No review of the main student journey by a person using a screen reader, keyboard only, 320px width or 200% zoom has been recorded, and there is no VPAT or ACR. The accessibility statement is a draft that claims no conformance ([draft](../../legal/ACCESSIBILITY-STATEMENT-DRAFT.md), [known limits](../../pilot/KNOWN-LIMITATIONS.md)).

## Symptom

You say one of these:

- "The text is too small."
- "There is too much movement on the screen."
- "I can't use this with my screen reader or keyboard."
- "The colours are hard to see."
- "A button has no name when I tab to it."

## Check

1. Your device's own settings apply first. Semester says: "Your device's own reduced-motion setting is always honoured."
2. Open **Settings** and look at the look settings. Text size and line spacing "move everything on every screen, including the tab bar. Nothing here is fixed in pixels." There is a **Text size** control, a **Movement** control and a **Workspace mode** control.
3. Check **Workspace mode**. Its note reads "Accessibility turns on larger text, more space and less motion." None of the modes changes what you can do.

## Fix

1. In **Settings**, set **Text size** and line spacing to what you can read.
2. Set **Movement** to a calmer option if the animation bothers you.
3. In **Workspace mode** choose **Accessibility** to apply larger text, more space and less motion together.
4. Choosing Accessibility also sets the text, spacing and motion settings themselves, so you can see its effect there and undo each one separately.
5. Contrast: the colour settings include a contrast readout.
6. If something is still blocked, report it. Write to the support address shown under Settings, then About, and put "Accessibility" in the subject. Say which screen, what you were trying to do, and what happened. Add your browser and assistive technology. The reply says what was found and what happens next. No response time is promised yet.
7. If an accessibility reply does not resolve it, write again with "Accessibility escalation" in the subject. That message goes to the accessibility seat. See [who holds that seat](../README.md#staffing-today).
8. When support tickets are switched on, the category **An accessibility barrier** is available. It is the same report through the in-app form.

## Not your fault

- **A barrier is Semester's to fix, not yours to work around.** Automated checks do not find all of them, and nobody has recorded a manual review yet. A barrier that stops you finishing what you came to do is meant to be fixed before anything else in that screen ships ([known limits](../../pilot/KNOWN-LIMITATIONS.md)).
- **Your school's other systems.** A barrier in your school's learning system or registration page belongs to your school's accessibility office, not to Semester.
- **Accessibility offices and money offices are reached directly.** In the help-request feature they are directory-only: Semester points to the office and sends and stores nothing about it. See the Support screen.

## Contact

### What to send

- The screen, and what you were trying to do.
- The browser and version, and the assistive technology you used (name and version, if you know it).
- What happened and what you expected.
- Whether the settings above helped.

### What not to send

- Medical or disability documentation. You do not need to prove anything to report a barrier.
- Passwords, student ID numbers, or screenshots of grades.
- Do not test against another student's account.
