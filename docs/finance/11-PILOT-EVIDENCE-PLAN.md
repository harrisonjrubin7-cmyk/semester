## Why these three, and what they are worth

Scenario 8 is the plan that respects the go/no-go decision: no paid pilots, no direct enterprise contracts and no broad student acquisition until their gates. Holding Year-3 ARR at $2,954k, one input at a time:

| Input moved | Setting | Year-3 ARR | Change | Peak funding need |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Pilot-to-annual conversion | 0% (plan 50%) | $2,306k | -$648k (-22%) | $23.4M |
| | 25% | $2,630k | -$324k (-11%) | $23.1M |
| | 75% | $3,278k | +$324k (+11%) | $22.7M |
| Institutional new-logo volume | 60% of plan | $2,208k | -$746k (-25%) | $23.4M |
| | 140% of plan | $3,700k | +$746k (+25%) | $22.4M |
| Enterprise signing delay | 3 months | $2,099k | -$855k (-29%) | $23.3M |
| | 6 months | $1,844k | -$1,110k (-38%) | $23.4M |
| Every gate slips | 6 months | $1,919k | -$1,035k (-35%) | $23.5M |
| | 12 months | $1,210k | -$1,744k (-59%) | $23.8M |

Reading it:

1. **Timing moves the answer more than conversion or volume.** A six-month signing delay costs more ARR than losing 40% of the logos, and a twelve-month slip of every gate costs more than losing all pilot conversion. The unit that matters is therefore *how long it takes to get from a first conversation to a signed agreement*, and it is the one the repository has least evidence on.
2. **Conversion is the cheapest to measure and the cheapest to be wrong about.** Each 25 points is $324k of Year-3 ARR. It also takes the longest to read, because the first reading needs a pilot to finish its 26 weeks.
3. **Peak funding need barely moves with any of them** (about $22.4M to $23.8M against $22.9M) because the cost side is fixed by hiring. The evidence changes *revenue and the case for raising the next round*, not the size of the first tranche. That is why the first tranche is sized by gated hiring ([`12-GATED-HIRING-SCHEDULE.md`](12-GATED-HIRING-SCHEDULE.md)), not by these inputs.

The model has **no explicit sales-cycle input.** Sales-cycle length enters only through the gate months (A-162 for the first paid pilot, A-163 and A-164 for direct sales) and the signing-delay driver S-06. Measuring it therefore updates those inputs, not a cell labelled "sales cycle". If the founder wants an explicit input, that is a model change to ask for.

## What may be done today, and only that

The go/no-go decision authorizes, for institutions: **discovery, synthetic demonstrations, evidence exchange, fit and limitation review and conditional scoping**, with no live data, no payment, no tenant activation, no customer or logo claim and no launch promise. It conditionally authorizes **invitation-only, unpaid individual validation** for one named cohort once its conditions are accepted. A **single bounded design-partner pilot** may follow only after a signed GO. A paid pilot, accepting payment and broad enterprise sale are NO-GO.

Everything below is built from those authorizations. Where a measure cannot be read inside them, the plan says it must wait.

| Measure | What it needs | Readable now? | Earliest authorized source |

| Institutional logo volume (qualified conversations and design-partner candidates) | Discovery conversations | **Yes**, as a pipeline funnel | Discovery |
| Sales-cycle length (conversation to *written intent to pilot*) | Discovery conversations with dated steps | **Partly**: the early stages only | Discovery; the signed-agreement stage waits for a pilot |
| Pilot-to-annual conversion | A finished 26-week pilot with a decision record | **No** | One bounded design-partner pilot after a signed GO, then its measured closeout |

### Measure 1: institutional logo volume

**Definition.** Count institutions at each stage, never people or meetings:

| Stage | Entry criterion | Evidence in the log |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S0 Contacted | A named role at the institution has replied | Role, institution type (no names in the log; the institution is recorded in a separate pipeline sheet kept by the founder) |
| S1 Qualified conversation | A role with a buying or sponsoring interest has had a discovery conversation about a stated job to be done | One `DE-` entry per question asked |
| S2 Fit reviewed | Synthetic demonstration held; fit and limitations written down and shared | Entry plus the shared limitations document |
| S3 Candidate | The institution says in writing it would consider a bounded design-partner pilot, conditionally | A written note from the sponsor |
| S4 Signed GO | Design-partner activation record approved under the go/no-go conditions | The activation record |

**What the model needs from it.** The plan's volume is the input A-025 (4, 10 and 14 pilots signed a year) and the multiplier S-04 on direct logos. What discovery can tell the founder is the *ratio* of S3 to S1: how many qualified conversations it takes to produce one candidate. A year-1 pilot count is not readable from discovery, because a pilot is not authorized to start before the signed GO.

**Honest limits.** Early institutions are chosen, not sampled; a funnel from conversations the founder could get is an upper bound on the funnel from conversations the founder cannot. Report it that way.

### Measure 2: sales-cycle length

**Definition.** Elapsed calendar days between consecutive stages for each institution, from the dated log entries. The headline is the *median* days from S1 to S3, with the range and the count. A mean hides one very long institution.

**What the model needs from it.** The plan implies, with a design-partner pilot at the first paid-pilot gate (month 12, A-162) and go-live two months after signing (A-030), that the company will be ready to be paid from about October 2027. Whether an institution's own procurement needs more or less time than the plan allows is exactly what the S1-to-S3 median will suggest, in the sense of "does a candidate stay in the funnel through one academic term". Read the median against the academic calendar: an institution that is quiet over a break is not a long cycle, and the log should say which dates were breaks.

**Honest limits.** S1 to S3 omits the longest part of an institutional cycle (security review, legal, procurement). Treat it as a *lower bound* on the cycle to a signed agreement. The upper stages become readable only through the design-partner pilot's own paperwork.

### Measure 3: pilot-to-annual conversion

**Two denominators, kept apart.**

* The repository defines it as pilots whose midpoint report led to a signed annual agreement, as a share of pilots that **reached the midpoint** ([`COMPANY-FIRST-YEAR-MEASURES.md`](../COMPANY-FIRST-YEAR-MEASURES.md)).
* The model's A-031 applies the share to pilots **signed**. A pilot that stops before the midpoint is a non-conversion in the model and is excluded in the repository measure.

Record both numerators and both denominators. Update the model only with the one that matches its denominator (converted over signed); report the repository figure alongside it.

**What counts as a conversion.** A signed annual agreement for the piloted scope, with the written decision from the closeout meeting ([`PILOT-TO-ANNUAL-CONVERSION.md`](../PILOT-TO-ANNUAL-CONVERSION.md)). A verbal intent, an extension of the pilot or a "convert" decision without a signature is not a conversion. Pause and stop are recorded as they happened.

**What is readable, and when.** The first design-partner pilot, if it is signed and activated, closes out after 26 weeks of live running and a decision meeting. Until then the measure has no reading. Even then the first reading is **one pilot: 0% or 100%**.

## Reading small numbers without fooling ourselves

With only a handful of pilots, a percentage is a coincidence of one or two institutions. A 95% Wilson interval for a conversion rate:

| Pilots signed | Converted | Observed | 95% interval |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 1 | 1 | 100% | 21% to 100% |
| 3 | 1 | 33% | 6% to 79% |
| 5 | 0 | 0% | 0% to 43% |
| 5 | 2 | 40% | 12% to 77% |
| 10 | 5 | 50% | 24% to 76% |
| 10 | 2 | 20% | 6% to 51% |

Two consequences:

1. **No reading below five finished pilots may change the model's conversion input.** One to four pilots can describe what happened and why; they cannot move 50% to anything else. Record them as cases.
2. **Five finished pilots is the first count at which a bad reading can be told from the plan:** zero converted of five rules out 50% at 95%. A good reading of five cannot rule out much (2 of 5 is consistent with 12% to 77%). The plan can therefore be *falsified* early and only *confirmed* late. That asymmetry is a reason to keep the first tranche sized to survive a bad reading.

Five finished pilots is far later than the model's Year-1 start, because in scenario 8 no pilot is signed before month 12 and each takes 26 weeks. That is a **timing fact about the go/no-go decision**, not a flaw in this plan: the decision chose to learn slowly in return for not taking payment or live data before the gates.

1. **Change one cell at a time** and re-run the guard so the effect of each reading is visible.
2. **Move the cell, then move the gate.** A reading that changes conversion does not by itself release a hiring gate; gates are released by their own evidence ([`12-GATED-HIRING-SCHEDULE.md`](12-GATED-HIRING-SCHEDULE.md)).

## What would change this plan

* If counsel decides interviews need consent or review, phase A starts later and every date moves with it.
* If no institution reaches S3 after ten qualified conversations, the volume question is already answered more cheaply than by waiting for pilots: the hypothesis of four pilots a year is in trouble, and the board pack should say so before the next financing conversation.
* If a prospect asks to pay, the answer is the go/no-go decision's: no payment is accepted until the paid-pilot gate. The plan does not test price by taking money early.
