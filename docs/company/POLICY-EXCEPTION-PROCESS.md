## Purpose and principle

An exception is a temporary, explicit decision—not an undocumented workaround. It cannot authorize unlawful conduct, deceptive claims, unknown customer/data authority, hidden accessibility exclusion, unsupported high-impact AI, or bypass of a required outside approval.

## Request

Record:

- exception ID, requester, policy/control and exact requirement;
- business/customer need and why compliant alternatives are insufficient;
- scope, systems, data, users, environments and affected claims;
- risk assessment, legal/contract/customer constraints;
- compensating controls and monitoring;
- start, expiry and remediation/exit plan;
- evidence, owner/back-up and approvals required.

## Review and decision

Domain owners evaluate security, privacy, accessibility, reliability, legal, customer and financial impact. The decision is **APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS**, **REJECT**, or **RETURN FOR INFORMATION**. Approval records conditions, prohibited interpretations, residual risk, monitoring, notification, expiry and revocation trigger.

No requester approves their own exception. High/critical risk requires independent domain review and the authority defined in the matrix. Customer or provider exceptions require their authorized acceptance where applicable.

## Monitoring and closure

Review active exceptions at least monthly and before release, sale, activation or renewal. Revoke on failed conditions, incident, expanded scope, evidence expiry, legal/contract change or safer alternative. At expiry, remediate, reapprove from current evidence, or stop the affected activity. Closure records verification and updates related risks/claims/documents.

## Emergency exception

During an incident, the incident commander may authorize the minimum reversible deviation allowed by law/contract and the approved authority matrix. Record it immediately, monitor continuously, notify required authorities and complete retrospective review within `[TBC]` days.

## Cannot be completed from source code

Named authorities, thresholds, current policies, live requests, legal/customer constraints, risk acceptance, monitoring and closure evidence require operating records and approval.
