## Purpose

Translate Semester's strategy into a financed, owned, evidence-based operating plan. This draft defines the planning structure; it does not invent targets, revenue, headcount, customers, dates, or readiness.

## Strategic posture

Semester's near-term objective is to prove a trustworthy student first win and a bounded institutional first proof, then earn the right to expand. The operating sequence is:

1. close company/legal/operating foundations;
2. make the core individual journey safe, accessible, reliable, and truthfully described;
3. qualify one design partner and freeze a narrow pilot;
4. complete target-environment, professional, and customer acceptance;
5. operate, measure, convert, extend, or offboard based on evidence;
6. scale only after repeatability and capacity are demonstrated.

## Quarterly sequencing

### Q1 — Foundation and truth

Confirm company facts/owners; complete counsel/accounting/insurance intake; reconcile legal/trust/operations packages; resolve contradictory release evidence; close launch-critical product/security/accessibility/reliability gaps; qualify a design partner without activating.

### Q2 — Named-environment acceptance

Freeze the pilot; execute paper; configure the target; complete role/isolation, accessibility, security, monitoring, support, recovery, data-rights, and UAT evidence; convene launch council.

### Q3 — Controlled operation

Activate only on signed GO; operate weekly evidence reviews; monitor guardrails/support; hold midpoint and executive reviews; capture limitations and remediation without expanding scope casually.

### Q4 — Decision and repeatability

Convert, extend under explicit terms, or offboard; reconcile outcomes and permissions; improve the operating system; decide whether evidence supports a second pilot, individual expansion, or continued constraint.

Quarter labels are sequencing placeholders, not promised dates. Reforecast against actual authority, funding, team capacity, and external-review lead times.

## Commercial assumptions register

Record buyer, use case, cohort, duration, scope, implementation, price, payment, conversion, support, cost, and outcome assumptions with evidence state: proposed, tested, verified, rejected, or expired. Never convert a proposed price, pipeline stage, or target outcome into an actual.

## Operating scorecard

Each metric must name the event/system source, owner, cadence, cohort, privacy threshold, interpretation caveat, baseline, target authority, and response trigger. Prioritize meaningful workflow completion, retention, satisfaction, implementation quality, support burden, safety/quality guardrails, and outcome evidence—not logins alone.

## Risk, scenario, and runway triggers

Maintain base/downside/upside scenarios with cash, burn, runway, committed costs, hiring, vendor, legal-review, and pilot assumptions. Define founder-approved triggers for spending freeze, scope reduction, vendor change, hiring, financing action, pilot pause, and business-continuity activation.
