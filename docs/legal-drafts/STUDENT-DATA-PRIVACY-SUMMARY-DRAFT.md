# Student Data Privacy Summary — Draft

This plain-language draft is not approved, published, or in force and is not a FERPA, COPPA, state-law, contractual, or institutional-compliance claim.

## Intended summary for students

Semester is designed to help a student organize academic work while limiting unnecessary collection and sharing. Some information can remain in the student's browser or device. Features that synchronize data, connect to an institution, use AI, process payments, or contact support may send defined information to a service provider or institution. The exact behavior depends on the feature and approved deployment configuration.

## What students should be told before use

- What information the feature needs and why.
- Whether the information stays on the device, is synchronized to Semester infrastructure, goes to a named provider, or is shared with an institution.
- Which party controls the information and where to direct access, correction, export, deletion, or complaint requests.
- How long each category is kept and which legal, security, billing, or institutional obligations may limit deletion.
- Whether an AI system is involved, what context is sent, and what limitations apply.
- Whether an institution can view information, under which role and authorization, and how that access is logged or revoked.

## Proposed privacy commitments

Semester should collect and disclose only what is necessary for an approved purpose; avoid behavioral advertising and undisclosed profiling; separate tenants; protect sensitive academic, accessibility, support, and basic-needs information; and provide understandable controls for eligible access, export, correction, deletion, and sharing.

No statement that content is never retained, never used for training, never shared, or always stored only on-device may be made across all features without scope-specific evidence. Provider terms and institutional configurations can change the correct disclosure.

## AI, advertising, and improvement

Education records and private student content must not be represented as available for advertising or general-purpose model training. Any proposed exception requires a separately documented purpose, legal basis, agreement, notice or authorization where required, and technical controls. De-identified or aggregated measurement must be defined and reviewed rather than assumed anonymous.

## Student choices and rights

The effective notice must explain available product controls and legally required rights for the student's jurisdiction and deployment. It must not promise a universal right, deadline, or deletion result that the actual process and applicable agreement cannot deliver. If an institution is the responsible data controller or record custodian, the notice must clearly identify the institution's role and request route.

## Behavior and evidence mapping

The detailed data inventory, flows, retention schedule, rights workflow, deletion behavior, support-access rules, AI provider handling, and subprocessors are maintained in the trust and market-readiness records. Repository evidence can show intended technical behavior; production configuration, institutional instructions, executed terms, and operating records must separately substantiate the live disclosure.

## Publication blockers

- [ ] Counsel and the privacy owner approve data roles, jurisdictions, age thresholds, notices, consent, rights, and complaint language.
- [ ] The data inventory and flow are verified against the exact production scope.
- [ ] Retention, export, deletion, support access, AI handling, and subprocessor statements match observed behavior and executed terms.
- [ ] Each institution approves its role, contact route, permitted purpose, and student-facing language.
- [ ] A plain-language and accessibility review is complete.

Until then, this summary is review material only and must not be used as a public privacy assurance.
