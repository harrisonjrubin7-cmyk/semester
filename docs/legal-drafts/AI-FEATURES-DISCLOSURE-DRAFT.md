## Plain-language notice

Semester includes optional AI-assisted workflows. AI can be wrong, incomplete or misleading. Check important information against original sources, your instructor and official institutional systems. AI output is not an official academic record, professional advice, grade, disciplinary decision, eligibility decision or substitute for human judgment.

## When information is sent

Information is sent only through an AI action the user or authorized institution invokes, using the context shown or approved for that workflow. The exact provider and terms depend on whether the connection uses Semester's configured provider, the user's own account/key, or an institution-approved gateway. Provider identity, data sent, purpose, retention/training terms and available alternative must be disclosed before use.

## User and institutional control

- Users may use non-AI/manual workflows where available.
- Course and institution rules can restrict AI use; users remain responsible for academic-integrity requirements.
- Institutions must separately approve provider, data scope, configuration, roles and contract terms.
- High-impact academic, financial, accessibility, safety, conduct, admission, employment or eligibility decisions require authorized human decision-makers and are outside the default AI role.

## Data and output boundaries

Do not send secrets, unnecessary sensitive data, another person's information without authority, or content prohibited by course/customer policy. Semester must minimize context, enforce classification/provider rules, avoid model training where the governing terms prohibit it, and provide deletion/retention information accurately. Source-grounded workflows must preserve citations and label uncertainty; generated content remains subject to review.

## Known limitations

Models can fabricate citations/facts, reproduce bias, misunderstand context, vary after provider/model changes and fail on inaccessible or poor-quality source material. Automated safeguards reduce but do not eliminate risk. No universal accuracy, fairness, safety or bias-free claim is made.

## Reporting and recourse

Users must be able to report incorrect, harmful, biased, inaccessible or policy-conflicting output and continue through an appropriate non-AI or human route. `[AI SUPPORT/PRIVACY/ACCESSIBILITY CONTACTS AND RESPONSE PROCESS TO BE APPROVED]`.

## Required approval evidence

Current AI-system/provider inventory; data-flow/classification and permitted-purpose review; provider contracts and retention/training terms; prompt/model/change controls; red-team/evaluation results for the use case; accessibility and human-oversight test; incident/kill switch; course/institution policy; user notice/consent where required; named owners and monitoring.

## Prohibited claims

Do not claim that AI is always accurate, unbiased, safe, private, compliant, explainable, institution-approved, instructor-approved, non-training or zero-retention without current scope-specific evidence. Do not imply that an AI recommendation is a human or official decision.

## External decisions

Applicable notices/consent, age posture, provider terms, institution/course authority, record retention, intellectual-property treatment and jurisdiction-specific AI obligations require qualified review.

## Publication blockers

Approved AI-system inventory; provider/contract/data-flow reconciliation; user and institution notices; course/age/jurisdiction review; human-oversight and accessible alternative operation; target-environment kill-switch/incident exercise; and confirmation that every public statement matches the deployed provider, model, prompt and retention/training terms.
