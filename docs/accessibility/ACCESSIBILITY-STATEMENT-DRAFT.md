# Accessibility at Semester

Semester is for every student, and for the people who support them: guardians, advisors, faculty, staff and
alumni. We want every one of them to be able to use it, with the tools and settings they already use.

## Our standard

We are working to meet the **Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2, Level AA**. We are building to
that standard in the design, the code and the tests of every change, not checking it at the end.

## Where we are

*[Edit to the evidence at the time of publishing. Today the true version is:]*

- Every change we make is checked automatically for the things a machine can check: names on controls,
  headings and landmarks, keyboard focus, colour contrast, text size, reduced motion, and error messages.
- **We have not yet completed an evaluation by people using screen readers and other assistive technology,
  and we have not published a conformance report.** We say so plainly. *[Replace with the dates, the
  technologies and versions tested, and the result, once a pass has been recorded.]*
- We expect to complete an independent evaluation by *[date]* and to publish the report here.

## What you can change

Settings → Appearance and Reading, on any device, for anyone, with no need to ask:

- Text size, line spacing, and spacing between letters and words
- Reading width and a reading ruler *[when built]*
- Light, dark, and high-contrast themes; works with Windows forced colours
- Less motion and Low stimulation
- Simple-language wording *[when built]*, and read-aloud
- Dates and numbers in your own language *[when built: interface language]*

## Known limitations

*[Fill from the open ledger at the time of publishing. Each item has a workaround, an owner and a date.
Today, from the program page, the honest list of areas not yet evaluated is:]*

- Rich-text editors, data tables, charts and media controls have not yet been checked with screen readers.
- Captions are not yet confirmed on every audio and video item, and audio description and live captions on
  calls are not yet in place.
- The interface is in English only.
- Content your school uploads (documents, slides, videos) is the school's, and may not be accessible; we flag
  what we can find and you can ask for an accessible version.
- Embedded tools from other providers are theirs; where we know their accessibility, we say so.

## Ask for an accessible version, or report a problem

You do not need to give a reason, and you do not need an account.

- **In the app:** Help → *Report an accessibility problem*, or *Request an accessible version* on the item.
- **By email:** *[accessibility address: not yet created]*
- **By phone or text relay:** *[number: not yet created]*
- **Through your school:** your school's accessibility office can file for you.

We reply within **one business day**. We aim to fix problems within these times:

| How serious | What it means | We aim to fix it within |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Critical | You cannot do something you need to do | 5 business days, and a workaround in 2 |
| Serious | You can, but only with great effort | 30 days |
| Moderate | There is a clear way around it | 90 days |
| Minor | A smaller improvement | Our next major release |

*[Publish these times only if the accessibility lead has accepted them. A longer honest time beats a missed one.]*

If a problem blocks something with a deadline, tell us it has a deadline. We treat it as more urgent, and we tell
your school so it can extend the deadline while we fix it.

## If you are not satisfied

Tell us. If we have not answered well, you can ask your school's accessibility coordinator, or *[the
appropriate route as approved by counsel]*.

We design for current versions of Chrome, Firefox, Safari and Edge on Windows, macOS, iOS and Android, and for the
screen readers NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver and TalkBack, and for Voice Control, Dragon, switch access and screen
magnifiers. *[Edit to the combinations actually tested.]*
