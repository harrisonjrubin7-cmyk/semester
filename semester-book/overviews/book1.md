## Executive summary

Semester describes itself as the student action platform: one daily workspace that turns a student's semester into the next clear step, with the source of every fact shown beside it. The README also calls it the native Student Operating System for the whole educational journey, with workspaces for faculty, advisors, registrars, finance and IT or security staff. "Student OS" is internal only, because it implies replacement. The product works alongside a school's official systems (SIS, LMS, identity provider). It reads from them, labels each fact as official, connected, personal or AI-assisted, and shows when it last synced.

The company is Harrison Rubin's: a solo-founder LLC at pre-seed, bootstrapped, with no revenue. Contractors work under signed IP assignments. As of October 2026 a first paid pilot is signed with Vanderbilt University, but pilot start and renewal-decision dates are not set. The Operating Pack lists six launch blockers that must close before real student records arrive: cyber insurance, E&O insurance, DPA/FERPA obligation mapping, HECVAT Full, SAML SSO and a VPAT/ACR. It counts 23 open P0 items. Of its 56 checklist items, only LLC formation, IP assignments and the signed pilot agreement are marked done.

The target customer is a four-year university of 5,000 to 30,000 students. The champion is a director of student success, advising or the registrar; the economic buyer is a VP of Student Success, the provost's office or the CIO. The wedge offer is the Registration Readiness Pilot: one term, one cohort, named success measures, SSO and a read-only SIS sync. It targets students surprised by holds and missed registration steps, and overloaded advisors. The GTM plan's strategy in one line: universities first, through one paid one-term pilot per institution, with a renewal decision date. Vanderbilt is pilot #1 and its results are meant to become the case study that sells pilots #2 to #5. Free pilots are not allowed.

The business model has several planned streams, all priced as planning assumptions, not published prices. The student workspace is free at participating schools; Student Premium is planned at $8.99 a month or $69 a year. The institution platform is planned at $18 per enrolled student per year with a $30,000 minimum. Other lines are implementation ($35,000 to $150,000, waived or credited for early pilots), premium support, AI overage and a 12% marketplace commission. The pilot fee is quoted per pilot and credited against year one if the institution converts within 60 days of results. No source states the Vanderbilt fee; the Finance Model leaves it as "[signed fee]".

Go-to-market is founder-led. The 12-month targets are a 40-account target list, 15 discovery conversations, 10 demos, 6 pilot designs, 5 security and legal reviews, 3 signed pilots (including Vanderbilt) and 2 annual conversions. The sources say plainly that these are founder planning numbers, sized to one person selling alongside delivery.

The company is meant to run on the discipline it sells. The Company OS defines 15 business functions, each with an owner, system of record, priority and next action, plus a cadence and a KPI tree whose north star is students who complete their next action each week in the pilot cohort. The founder owns all 15 today. Hiring is revenue-triggered: no role opens until its trigger is true and signed revenue covers its cost. The setup budget is $5,000 to $20,000; the Finance Model's three scenarios total $4,873, $12,430 and $19,910, with a run-rate of about $565 a month and a $0 founder salary.

The brand stance is calm, precise and source-aware: "a good registrar's assistant crossed with a precision instrument." The company states status, not certifications it does not hold (no "SOC 2 compliant", no "FERPA certified"). The visual system uses near-black Ink chrome, warm Parchment content and one restrained Semester blue for action; every status carries a glyph and a word. A standing product rule is that student records never reach an AI provider.

The strategy documents are labelled "design-stage evidence only" and score platform maturity at an average of 1.16 out of 5. Several handoff artefacts are described as not yet run or tested. This overview reports what the founder's documents plan and claim; it does not verify that any of it exists in running code.

## How the company works

### Operating system and cadence

The Company OS (dated 2026-10-05) covers 15 functions: governance and legal; finance and capital; product and engineering; trust (security, privacy, accessibility, compliance); revenue; marketing; customer success; support; partnerships; people; data and outcomes; vendors; risk and continuity; board and investors; and the operations command center. Every owner is the founder, sometimes paired with counsel, a bookkeeper or a fractional security lead. The principle: Semester must operate with the same coherence, evidence, reliability and trust it promises institutions.

Everything is meant to run in Semester's own `/ops` console. Regulated systems stay external and embedded: bank, payment processor, payroll, the bookkeeper's ledger, e-signature, GitHub, Vercel and Supabase. The product database tracks quotes, orders, invoices and entitlements but is not the statutory ledger.

The cadence runs from daily operations triage to weekly reviews (revenue, product, customer success, security and reliability, marketing), monthly finance and trust reviews, and a quarterly OKR and board review. While solo, the weekly reviews collapse into one 60-minute operating review. The Operating Manual's week: Monday planning and pipeline review, Wednesday engineering review, Thursday pilot check-in with the champion, Friday metrics, cash and risks. Monthly there is a two-hour close with access and claims reviews; quarterly, a half-day reset and a tabletop exercise. Decisions are logged one per pull request in DECISIONS.md, yet the backlog still lists a company decisions log as a gap. The Master Backlog sorts functions P0 to P3 and adds a 90-day plan, a 12-month plan, a 36-month plan and a top-50 action list.

### KPIs

The north star is students who complete their next action each week (pilot cohort). Seven branches sit beneath it: product (weekly actives, first meaningful action, readiness completion), revenue (pipeline, pilots, conversion, ARR, NRR), customer (activation, health, measures met), trust (controls with evidence, incidents), finance (cash, runway, margin, AI cost per workflow), reliability (SLO attainment, restore time) and people (capacity versus demand). The pilot one-pager names three example measures: students with no unresolved hold at registration time, advising appointments arriving with an agenda, and weekly active students completing a next action. Baselines and targets are "agreed at kickoff"; no source states them.

### Finance and capital

Finance must answer cash and runway, committed versus collected revenue, per-customer profitability, the margin effect of AI and infrastructure, and what the company can afford next. The quote-to-cash flow runs quote, pricing-exception check, order form, invoice, reconciliation, entitlement, revenue-recognition review and renewal. Discounts need founder approval until a deal desk exists; credits and refunds need finance approval.

The Finance Model gives three setup scenarios, each with a 10% contingency; base is recommended. Line items are counsel review, cyber plus E&O premiums, accessibility testing, accounting setup, tools, hosting, AI usage and campus travel; a pen test appears only in the full scenario. The monthly run-rate is mostly insurance if paid monthly ($250), bookkeeping ($150) and AI usage ($50). The model assumes the pilot invoice is paid about three months after setup starts and recommends asking for 50% at signature. Moving to Supabase Team for a vendor SOC 2 report would add about $574 a month. Fundraising is deferred until pilot results; an LLC-to-Delaware C-corp conversion is planned before any priced round. The documents flag that accounting, tax, legal and insurance content needs qualified professional review.

### People and hiring

Only the founder exists today. Fractional counsel, an insurance broker and a bookkeeper (about two hours a month) are needed now, and an accessibility tester before launch. A founding full-stack engineer comes after the pilot fee arrives; an implementation and customer success lead after the second pilot; a fractional security lead after the first annual contract; an account executive and product designer after three annual contracts; a support specialist last. Six job descriptions and four contractor briefs are written. Every hire gets confidentiality and IP assignment before access, FERPA and security training, and least-privilege access reviewed quarterly. Twelve cultural standards include student agency over surveillance, evidence over hype, and every public claim has evidence. Gaps: handbook, equity plan, compensation bands.

### Governance, legal and risk

No contract is signed without a template or counsel redline. Counsel is required for any contract with student data, liability above fees or non-standard indemnity. Draft MSA, Order Form, DPA, Terms, Privacy Policy, Cookie Notice, Accessibility Statement, Community Guidelines and Subprocessors exist, all awaiting attorney review. Gaps: SLA, SOW, security addendum, contractor templates, trademark filing and a policy register.

The Business Risk Register lists ten risks. Customer concentration (one pilot) and founder capacity are rated high on both likelihood and impact. The rest include pilot without insurance, runway shortfall, vendor breach, unsupported promises, accessibility complaints, prices too low to fund support and over-claiming. The key-person continuity plan is not yet written; the Security Policies describe a sealed emergency kit with a named backup contact. Every vendor needs a purpose, a review and an exit; reviews for Supabase, Vercel, the AI provider and the email provider are not yet recorded.

### Go-to-market and revenue

Two engines: institutional (paid pilots, annual contracts, expansion, references) and student adoption (activation, retained use, institutional proof). The funnel has 15 stages with exit criteria, from Target to Closed lost. The GTM plan holds the ICP, six personas with promises and objections, a ten-line offer ladder, pipeline targets and a case-study plan: sign measures before launch, ask permission at the midpoint review, publish a two-page study within 30 days of results. Disqualifiers: no named champion, a need for SIS write-back during the pilot, or procurement over six months. Claim rule: say "in a paid pilot with one university" until there are more.

Market Research supports positioning, not forecasting. It names EAB Navigate360, Starfish, Civitas Learning and AI-native entrants, all with vendor-claimed scale, and positions Semester as complementing Navigate and Starfish on the staff side in year one rather than replacing them. Gaps: content calendar, SEO plan, account scoring, ROI calculator, battlecards, procurement FAQ, demo environment, win/loss log.

### Customer success

Sales must hand off the signed scope, timeline, conversion date, sponsor, IT, security and procurement contacts, cohort, baseline and success criteria. A health score is proposed with example weights (student activation 20%, core workflow completion 20%, admin engagement 15%, first meaningful action 15%, and others), but its definition is a listed gap. The strategy adds an implementation factory (tenant templates, LTI and OneRoster wizards, SSO/SCIM guide, reconciliation dashboard) promising launch in weeks, not years.

### Partnerships

Partnerships are P3 and post-pilot. First partners are the pilot's own dependencies: SIS, LMS, identity provider and AI provider. Any data-sharing partner needs a DPA and review. The strategy sketches a developer platform, partner certification and a marketplace with governed installs.

### Trust program

Trust is meant to be evidence-based: each control has an owner, policy, implementation, evidence, test and review date. A new subprocessor needs privacy review and 30 days' notice. The P0 next action is to bind insurance, submit HECVAT Full and complete vendor reviews. Security detail is in the operations section below.

## Brand in brief

**Positioning.** The brand identity document says "Semester is the education operating system". The README and Operating Manual use "the student action platform" publicly. The sources do not reconcile this; the design system states it wins on conflicts. The intended student feeling is "I know what to do next."

**Personality.** Calm, not sterile; intelligent, not patronizing; ambitious, not inflated; academic, not bureaucratic; warm, not childish; human-centred, not surveillance-centred.

**Voice rules, with the sources' examples.**

- Use the status sentence: what is true, what it means for you, what you can do. "Your Canvas calendar hasn't updated since Tuesday. Deadlines below may have moved. Reconnect it, or check the course page."
- Prefer "helps you organise" to "revolutionises"; "alongside your school's systems" to "replaces" or "all-in-one"; "AI-assisted, source-linked" to "AI-powered" or "magic". Never claim "secure", "compliant" or "certified" without evidence.
- Say "student", not "user". Sentence case. No emoji, streaks, ranks, exclamation marks or confetti.
- State consequences before confirm: "You leave CHEM 101 and a W is recorded on your transcript. It cannot be undone here."
- Say what is safe in errors: "Your draft is safe on this device."
- Never call Semester the official gradebook, registrar or payment system. Label demo data "Sample".

**Visual identity.**

- *Colour.* Ink (#090a0e) for all chrome; Parchment (#f4f1ea) for content; Semester blue 700 (#416180) on light and 400 (#94bce3) on dark as the one action colour. Brass (#d8c79a) is editorial only; sterling (#d4d9e2) is the metal of the mark. Source hues are desaturated stones used as ink, hairline and 8% wash, never large fills.
- *Status glyphs.* ◆ official, ○ yours, ✦ AI, ⇄ connected or conflict, ? review, ! attention, ✓ done, ⊘ offline, … working, ◌ sample. Colour is never the only carrier.
- *Type.* Barlow body (16/1.55), Barlow Condensed 600 for headings and controls, Cinzel for rare editorial display and the wordmark, system mono for IDs. Tabular figures.
- *Shape and motion.* Corners 3/6/10 px; hairline first, shadow last. Motion 130 to 280 ms, zeroed by reduced-motion. No bounce, parallax or spinners.
- *Mark.* Three slabs on a 512 grid, shown as a lit silver mark on Ink. Never recolour it; in co-brand lockups Semester leads. No photography or AI imagery in-product. Icons: 108 glyphs, 1.5 px stroke.
- *Open decision.* The source PDF proposed indigo with emerald, amber and rose status hues; the design system kept blue and stone hues and flags this for the founder.

## How Semester is operated

**Support.** Four tiers: self-help, the customer's own help channel (Vanderbilt's, named by its IT), Semester's support desk run by the founder, and vendors. Severities S1 to S4: S1 is possible student-data exposure; S2 is blocked (SSO down, SIS data wrong); S3 is degraded; S4 is a question. Targets are in the numbers table. Tickets never contain student record content; they use internal IDs. Reply macros cover sign-in, wrong-looking data, "Is AI reading my records?", not-in-pilot, known issues and accessibility barriers. Week-1 cover ends after no open S1 or S2, two days without a new S2, clean reconciliation and sponsor agreement.

**On-call and SLOs.** The founder is on-call lead until a first engineer joins. Escalation runs on-call, founder, vendor support, institution IT. More than two night pages in a week makes the next week reliability work. Error budget policy: above 50% remaining, normal releases; 0 to 50%, fixes only; exhausted, freeze and write a reliability plan. Ten alerts map to runbooks; for example, 5xx above 2% for 15 minutes is an S2 and rollback trigger, and an RLS-denial spike from one session is an S1.

**Runbooks.** Eight follow detect, stabilise, fix, verify, tell people, write it down: R1 bad release, R2 SSO outage, R3 SIS sync (mismatches are never auto-corrected; the registrar decides the right source), R4 backup and restore (to a scratch project first), R5 suspected exposure (cohort flag off first, preserve evidence, postmortem in 5 business days), R6 AI gateway, R7 capacity, R8 a student who cannot see data. Maintenance is weekly patching, a monthly restore drill, quarterly access review and tabletop, and yearly pen test, ACR re-test and HECVAT refresh.

**Launch and cutover for the Vanderbilt pilot.** All dates are relative to launch day (L). Eleven go/no-go gates must be green with evidence: DPA signed (L-45), HECVAT accepted, insurance bound and ACR delivered (L-30), SSO in staging (L-21), SIS reconciliation at zero unexplained mismatches, RLS tests and AI-gateway red-team (L-14), restore drill (L-10), incident contacts and signed success measures (L-7). Final go/no-go and code freeze at L-3. The cutover plan has eleven steps: production migrations and RLS tests, secrets, Vanderbilt's SAML service provider, institution configuration, first SIS sync, a registrar spot-check of 25 students, DNS and TLS, baseline backup, staff accounts, fresh sync, and finally the student cohort flag. Each step has a check and an undo. There is no point of no return: until the cohort flag is on students see nothing, and afterwards it turns everything off in under a minute without deleting data. Rollback triggers: suspected exposure, SSO down over 30 minutes, SIS data shown wrongly, error rate above 2% for 15 minutes. Welcome messages come from Vanderbilt; nothing is public until both sides approve.

**Security readiness.** The policy set (v0.1 draft) has ten policies: access, encryption, AI and student data, vendors, incident response, BCP/DR, retention, secure development, devices and logging. Key commitments: MFA on every company account, RLS on every student-data table tested in CI, an AI gateway that blocks student-record fields before any model call, and deletion within 30 days of contract end. The readiness tracker holds draft HECVAT Full answers across 14 sections (mostly in progress), a SOC 2 table (Type I planned after the pilot), a staging-only pen-test scope and an insurance checklist (cyber, E&O, general liability, certificate; all not started). The platform security review found 11 issues, including a critical one letting a tenant user insert themselves as an ops role. Fixes are written but unrun and need a qualified security engineer.

**Compliance.** Stated status: HECVAT Full in progress, SOC 2 not started, no pen test, insurance not bound, VPAT/ACR in preparation. The DPA draft makes Semester a FERPA "school official" with no sale, advertising, profiling or AI training on institution data.

## Open decisions and risks

**Gating items before real student records.** Insurance is "not started" in the Operating Pack, readiness tracker and MSA draft, yet the pilot requires it. The DPA is "in review". HECVAT Full, SAML SSO and the VPAT/ACR are open. Also open: EIN and tax registrations, business bank account, accounting setup, subprocessor list, retention schedule, a tested AI boundary, data inventory and a tested restore.

**Decisions not yet made.**

- Pilot start and renewal dates, to be fixed in a mutual action plan.
- Pilot measures, baselines and targets, signed before launch.
- The Vanderbilt fee and invoice schedule (the Order Form offers 100% at signature, or 50% and 50%).
- Raise versus bootstrap, and C-corp conversion.
- Brand hues (blue and stone versus the PDF's indigo set).
- Whether to fund the pen test.
- When to publish prices, which wait for pilot results and counsel review.

**Top risks.** Customer concentration, founder capacity, runway before the pilot invoice, pilot without insurance, HECVAT delay, SSO readiness, student-record exposure (low likelihood, critical impact), VPAT gaps, a pilot drifting without a decision date, claims outrunning evidence, and prices too low to fund support. The Platform Risk Register adds cross-tenant exposure, AI receiving records and SIS data shown wrongly.

**Gaps from audits and backlogs.** The completion audit's top 25 include no production code for pilot paths, RLS untested, no monitoring, SIS connector unbuilt, AI red-team not run, no status page, no policy engine, no usability test, no 36-month model, no help centre, no event taxonomy and no decision log. The gap register lists 84 workflow steps without a screen. No maturity dimension scores above 2 of 5; developer ecosystem and global readiness score 0.

**Where the sources disagree.**

- *Accessibility standard.* The Operating Pack targets WCAG 2.1 AA for the ACR; the README, Accessibility Statement, MSA and GTM plan say 2.2 AA.
- *Launch blockers.* The Operating Pack's six differ from the launch kit's Command Center six (HECVAT, data agreement, SSO, accessibility, insurance as one item, and an incident plan).
- *Severity targets.* The Operating Manual uses P0 to P3 with 1 h, 4 h, 1 day and 2 day targets; the Support Plan uses S1 to S4 with 15 min, 30 min, 1 h and same day.
- *Restore drills.* Monthly in the Operations Handbook; quarterly in the Security Policies.
- *Breach notice.* 24 hours in Security Policies; a bracketed 72 hours in the DPA template.
- *Drafts versus "not started".* The Operating Pack lists the subprocessor list and a 12-month model as not started, though drafts exist in docs/legal and docs/run.
- *Pen-test timing.* After first renewal (Operating Pack) versus before the pilot goes broad (Security Policies).
- *First engineer.* After the pilot fee (Hiring Plan) versus after the second paid customer (Operating Manual).
- *Insurance risk.* Medium likelihood (Business Risk Register) versus High (Operating Pack).
- *Pilot term.* The Vanderbilt one-pager says "Fall 2026"; other documents say dates are not set.
- *Counts.* 49 versus 57 components.
