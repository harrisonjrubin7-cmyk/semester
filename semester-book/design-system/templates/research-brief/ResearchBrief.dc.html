SEMESTERResearch brief · October 2026

What a university checks before we touch a student record

Five forces shaping the Vanderbilt pilot and every deal after it: accessibility law, the security questionnaire, a major LMS breach, vendor instability and an unsettled AI picture.

Takeaways
  1. Build to WCAG 2.2 AA now. The federal deadline for public universities moved to April 2027, but the standard didn’t change, and some campuses already require 2.2.
  2. HECVAT 4 is the gate. 321 questions, now including privacy and AI. Every “No” needs a written explanation.
  3. The Canvas breach changed the questions buyers ask. They will want proof that one weak account can’t reach every tenant.
  4. Vendor survival is a buying criterion. After Anthology’s bankruptcy, expect a solo-founder company to be asked for an exit and data-return plan.
  5. No new federal AI rule for student records exists. Institutions are reading FERPA for themselves, so a per-data-type switch they control is the safest default.

1 · Accessibility: a deadline moved, the bar didn’t

On 20 April 2026 the Justice Department extended the ADA Title II web rule by a year. Public entities serving 50,000 or more people now have until 26 April 2027; smaller ones until 26 April 2028 (EDUCAUSE Review, June 2026). The standard remains WCAG 2.1 Level AA, and the rule covers course content and mobile apps as well as websites (EdTech Magazine).

Universities are telling staff not to slow down. UNC says expectations are unchanged and is holding to its own WCAG 2.2 AA standard, and tells staff who buy software to check accessibility during procurement (UNC Digital Accessibility).

Vanderbilt is private, so Title II doesn’t apply; private institutions fall under Title III and still face legal risk (Archer). Our read: public-university deals will ask for a VPAT against 2.1 AA; aiming at 2.2 AA covers both.

2 · Security review: HECVAT 4

EDUCAUSE released HECVAT 4 on 10 February 2025 and lists 4.1.6 as current (EDUCAUSE). It combines the old Full, Lite and On-Premise versions into one workbook and adds privacy and AI questions (EDUCAUSE Review). One guide counts 321 questions across seven sections, and says a “No” answer must explain the compensating safeguard (SaltyCloud).

Large universities are sharing assessments. UT Austin, UVA and Duke presented on running HECVAT 4 reviews at scale in April 2026 (EDUCAUSE CPP 2026). Our read: a complete, honest HECVAT is reusable sales material, not a one-off. Answer “planned” controls as planned, with a date.

3 · The Canvas breach

Instructure disclosed a breach on 1 May 2026, after detecting unauthorized activity on 29 April (Tech Insider). Instructure says the stolen data included names, email addresses, student ID numbers and messages between users, but no passwords or financial data (Wikipedia summary). The attackers came back on 7 May and defaced login pages. Instructure later confirmed both intrusions used a Free-For-Teacher account (ClassAction.org).

The scale figures disagree. The attackers claimed 3.65 TB and about 275 million users (UMD IT). Other reporting cites 231 million unique email addresses (Reed Smith). Both are attacker claims, not confirmed by Instructure. Trend Micro notes Canvas holds sensitive material such as accommodation requests, which makes targeted phishing the main risk.

Our read: expect questions on tenant isolation, free or self-serve accounts, and how fast we’d notify. Our rule that accommodations show without the reason, and that Marketplace apps never get records, is directly relevant evidence.

4 · Vendor instability: Anthology

Anthology, Blackboard’s parent, filed for Chapter 11 on 29 September 2025 (Davis Polk) with about $1.6 billion of debt, much of it from the 2021 Blackboard merger (ElevenFlo). Its SIS/ERP unit went to Ellucian and its student-success unit to Encoura. The sales closed on 31 December 2025 and 31 January 2026, and the remaining business now trades as Blackboard (Davis Polk).

Our read: institutions have just watched a major vendor break apart. A one-founder company should offer, before being asked, a documented data export, an escrow or exit clause, and a US-only hosting map.

The SIS market is more fragmented. Ellucian Banner leads with 24% and Workday holds 3% (ListEdTech, January 2025). Workday says it has more than 200 institutions under contract for Workday Student, a vendor figure (Full Fabric). Ellucian launched an “AI-native” Ellucian Student in April 2026 (MarketsandMarkets release).

Our read: incumbents are adding AI to the system of record. Semester’s edge is the student-facing layer across all of them, which supports the phased plan: connect read-only first, become authoritative only once proven.

6 · AI and FERPA: thin evidence

These searches found no new federal rule on AI and student records. AACRAO is preparing a 2026 FERPA publication that will cover AI and student privacy (AACRAO). The NEA notes that institutions must interpret FERPA themselves for how AI accesses, uses and stores data (NEA). HECVAT 4’s AI section is now the most concrete checklist buyers will use.

Gap: this is the weakest-sourced section. Before the pilot, ask Vanderbilt’s privacy office for its current AI-vendor guidance directly.
