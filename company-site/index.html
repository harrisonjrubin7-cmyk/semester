    Boundaries

    How Semester takes over, one module at a time.

    Runs beside your systems today. Takes each one over when you are ready. Being clear about status is part of the product: nothing below is built yet.

    • Today it does not register you for classes or guarantee a seat. Registration is the planned Core module that would.
    • Today it does not replace your SIS, LMS, degree audit or official records. Each is a planned module your school switches to Core, in writing, one at a time.
    • It does not make official academic decisions with AI.
    • It does not promise grades, retention, graduation or job outcomes.
    • It does not sell student data or show advertising.

                How we prioritize

                A weighted score, then a human decision.

                Each capability is scored 1–5 on every dimension. The score is a decision aid, not a replacement for strategy review. It is reviewed quarterly.

                Priority = Σ (score × weight)

                DimensionQuestionWeight
                Trust and risk reductionDoes it materially reduce security, privacy, accessibility or operational risk?+1.5
                Revenue enablementDoes it unlock a segment, contract or renewal?+1.3

                DependencyDoes it unblock several other capabilities?+1.2

                    Why I started Semester

                    A personal note from Harrison Rubin on the problem Semester exists to solve, and how that shaped the way it is built.

                    College is supposed to be a place where you build your future. But for a lot of students, it can feel like a constant process of trying to find the right information before it is too late.

                    I know that feeling personally.

                    As a transfer student, I had to navigate more than one institution at once. Important information lived in different systems, on different websites, in different emails, and with different people. I was trying to understand what would transfer, what I still needed, where I stood, what deadlines mattered, what courses made sense, and who I needed to talk to.

                    None of it was impossible. But it was exhausting.

                    Every answer seemed to create another question. Every school had its own portal, policies, course systems, forms, calendars, and language. The work of figuring out college often felt like a second course load—one that students are expected to manage alone.

                    Dyslexia made that experience even harder.

                    When information is spread across long documents, inconsistent websites, unreadable portals, emails, PDFs, course pages, and systems that do not connect, the problem is not simply finding information. It is holding everything together long enough to understand what it means.

                    I did not need more information.

                    I needed clarity.

                    I needed one place that could help me see where I was, what mattered next, what information I could trust, what I still needed to decide, and what I could do without losing control of my own work and data.

                    That is why I started Semester.

                    Semester is built on a simple belief: students should not have to become experts in university bureaucracy just to make progress in college.

                    A student should be able to understand their path. They should be able to plan their term, prepare for registration, organize coursework, find support, prepare for advising, build career evidence, and know what to do next—without having to piece their life together across a dozen disconnected systems.

                    Semester is not about replacing the people who help students. Advisors, faculty, tutors, librarians, disability-services professionals, career teams, and campus staff matter deeply. Semester is about making those conversations more useful by helping students arrive with context, questions, plans, and clearer next steps.

                    It is also not about hiding decisions behind AI.

                    Students deserve to know where information comes from. They deserve to know whether something is institution verified, imported, student entered, estimated, or needs review. They deserve to know what an AI tool is using, what it cannot know, and when an official decision still belongs with the institution or a person they trust.

                    Accessibility is personal to me, too.

                    Accessibility is not an extra feature. It is whether a student can read the screen, find the right action, navigate without getting lost, use a keyboard, listen to information, zoom in, take more time, understand the language, and complete the task in front of them.

                    A student should never feel like the system was built for someone else.

                    Semester is being built for the student who has too many tabs open.

                    The student who is transferring.

                    The student trying to understand requirements.

                    The student balancing work, family, athletics, health, commuting, or a new environment.

                    The student who needs information to be clearer, more connected, and easier to act on.

                    The student who is capable, but tired of fighting the systems that are supposed to help them succeed.

                    College is complicated.

                    Your path should not be.

                    Harrison Rubin — Founder, Semester

                    Personal principles

                    What I learned from being a transfer student

                    1. Information is not useful if students cannot find it when they need it.
                    2. A portal is not a solution if students still have to reconstruct their own story across multiple systems.
                    3. Accessibility changes whether a student can participate—not just how a page looks.
                    4. Students need clarity, not judgment.
                    5. Students deserve to know what information is official, what is estimated, and what needs review.
                    6. AI should make information easier to understand, not make decisions students cannot inspect or challenge.
                    7. Technology should help students have better conversations with people—not remove people from the process.

                      The final expansion test

                      Every new capability has to pass these questions.

                      1. Does it make a real student decision clearer?
                      2. Does it create a safe, measurable institutional benefit?
                      3. Can we explain source, authority, limitation and privacy impact?
                      4. Can we make it accessible?
                      5. Can we operate it during failure and academic peak periods?
                      6. Can we support it, contract it and offboard it?
                      7. Can we prove it works?
                      8. Would we be comfortable explaining it to a student, parent, faculty member, accessibility reviewer, privacy officer and regulator?

                      Operations

                      How we run Semester safely.

                      The rules our staff tools follow. The Company Operations Console that enforces them is in development and is not yet used with customer data; until it is, the same rules apply to how the founder works by hand.

                      Read-first by default

                      Views are read-only. Writes are limited, scoped, confirmed and audited, and show what will change and how to undo it.

                      Least privilege

                      Every role gets the narrowest access its job needs, with an expiry and a periodic review.

                      No unrestricted student data

                      No internal role, including the most privileged, gets unrestricted access to student data by default.

                      Access tiers

                      Five tiers, from read-only to break-glass.

                      Planned console access tiers
                      TierWhoCanCannot, by default
                      0 · Read-only insightExecutives, board or advisor viewersView approved aggregate reportsView student records, change configuration, grant access

                      4 · Break-glassOnly for an immediate security, safety, legal or recovery needTime-limited emergency accessAnything without the conditions below

                      Break-glass conditions

                      • A written emergency reason
                      • An incident or change ticket
                      • A time limit and automatic expiry
                      • Fresh multi-factor sign-in
                      • Two-person approval where feasible
                      • A prominent session banner
                      • An audit event for every read and write
                      • A mandatory review afterwards

                      Audit trail

                      Append-only, and nobody can erase their own.

                      Each audit event records who acted, their role, the tenant and scope, the action and target, the outcome, the reason and linked ticket, the source, a safe before-and-after reference and a server timestamp.

                      • Role grants and revocations
                      • Tenant, policy and feature-flag changes
                      • Support access opened and closed
                      • Student data export, deletion or correction
                      • Billing, credits and entitlement changes
                      • Releases, rollbacks and AI policy changes
                      • Every break-glass session

                      Support access

                      The student decides.

                      There is no generic “log in as a student.” If support ever needs to see an account:

                      • The student initiates or approves it
                      • It covers one support case and expires
                      • The session is visibly marked
                      • The agent states a reason, and every action is audited
                      • The student can revoke it at any time

                      Academic critical periods

                      Operations that know the academic calendar.

                      Registration

                      Source health, seat freshness, release freeze, integration alerts and support staffing.

                      Assessment & finals

                      Autosave and submission health, accommodations, load and incident coverage.

                      Grades

                      Gradebook latency, posting state and passback reconciliation.

                      Term start

                      Sign-in, roster sync, course publishing, onboarding and support volume.

                      Game days

                      We rehearse failure before it happens.

                      Each exercise records the scenario, owner, expected behavior and alert, what actually happened, time to detect and mitigate, and corrective actions with a retest date. The exercise program is planned; the restore drill is the first scheduled.

                      • Sign-in outage during registration
                      • LMS outage at a deadline
                      • Stale SIS feed
                      • AI provider outage
                      • Webhook replay
                      • Grade passback failure
                      • Network loss mid-assessment
                      • Feature-flag misconfiguration
                      • Accidental role grant
                      • Cross-tenant access regression
                      • Backup restore
                      • Production rollback

                      Incident learning

                      Every incident gets a blameless record: what happened, customer impact, timeline, root cause, contributing factors, what worked and what failed, corrective actions with owners and due dates, and the runbook and training changes that follow. The goal is never to fail the same way twice.

                      Governance cadence (planned)

                      • Weekly: incident, support and product triage
                      • Monthly: release quality, vendors and subprocessors, data quality
                      • Quarterly: accessibility, AI governance, security and privacy, customer advisory review

                      How AI works in Semester

                      Helpful, cited, never official.

                      AI features help you understand your own materials, study, draft non-official work and prepare questions for the right person. They don't make official determinations about registration, degrees, financial aid, accommodations, discipline, admissions, employment or health.

                      • Answers about your documents quote and cite the page, checked against the document
                      • AI output is never labelled institution-verified
                      • Every AI feature has an off switch
                      • You can report an answer with “Fix this”

                      Your choices and controls

                      • Export your data (today: your device's copy; full export in development)
                      • Delete your account and synced data
                      • Share with a specific person, for a set time, and revoke
                      • Disconnect connected accounts
                      • Choose notifications and quiet hours
                      • Turn AI features off
                      • Make a privacy request

                      What we don't do

                      • Sell personal information
                      • Load advertising or tracking SDKs (a test fails the build if one appears)
                      • Build behavioral, disciplinary or “risk” scores (the code refuses such metrics)
                      • Train models on your course materials or conversations without explicit authorization
                      • Hide choices or pressure you to share more
